# Travel documents for Brazilians

This guide summarizes what usually applies to people who hold **Brazilian citizenship only** and travel as tourists. Document rules change without notice: treat everything as a starting point and **confirm on the official site** (Federal Police, Itamaraty Consular Portal at gov.br/mre, the destination country's consulate, Receita Federal) before buying a ticket or boarding. People with dual citizenship, a visa from another country or residence abroad follow different rules. This material is planning guidance, not consular or legal advice. The rules for each destination are in the entry-by-destination guide.

The whole guide is specific to Brazil: it describes the Brazilian passport and ID documents, Brazilian law on minors traveling abroad (CNJ rules) and Brazilian customs and currency declarations (Receita Federal).

## Brazilian passport

Issued by the Federal Police (Polícia Federal, PF). The request starts online on gov.br: you fill in the form, pay the fee by GRU (federal payment slip) and only then book the in-person appointment (photo and biometrics).

- **Fee:** R$ 257.25 on the PF page checked on October 2, 2026; higher amounts apply for loss or emergency. Confirm the current amount on the PF site.
- **Validity:** 10 years for people over 18; shorter for children, depending on age.
- **Lead time:** the PF states 6 to 10 business days after the appointment, but the **appointment queue** varies by city and season (peaks before July and before December). Start at least **60 days before** the trip; at high demand, 90.
- **Emergency:** an urgent passport exists for proven cases (a booked trip, health, work), but do not count on it in your planning.

## Minimum passport validity by destination

The check happens first at the **airline**, at boarding, before the country's immigration.

- Many countries require **6 months of validity** counted from the date of entry.
- **Schengen area:** passport valid for at least **3 months after the planned date of departure** from the area and **issued less than 10 years ago**.
- Rule of thumb: if less than 6 months remain before it expires on the return date, recommend renewing before buying the ticket.
- A child needs their own passport for destinations outside Mercosur, with the same validity rule.

## Mercosur and associates: traveling with an ID only

Brazilians can enter as tourists with an identity document in Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, Chile, Bolivia, Peru, Colombia, Ecuador and Venezuela. Confirm the current list on the Consular Portal.

- **Accepted:** passport, state RG (the Brazilian identity card) or the new **National Identity Card (CIN)**, always the **physical** document.
- **Not accepted:** CNH (driver's license), professional council cards (OAB, CRM etc.), work card (carteira de trabalho), an adult's birth certificate, and the **digital** CIN or RG in the gov.br app.
- The RG must be in good condition, with a recognizable photo, and have been **issued at most 10 years ago**: this is the requirement applied by airlines and immigration in the region. An older RG is often turned away, even if it is still valid in Brazil (the transition period for the CIN runs until 2032).
- Children: their own RG or passport; a birth certificate is not accepted in most cases.

## Minors: travel authorization

Rule: **CNJ Resolution 131/2011** (CNJ is Brazil's National Council of Justice) and its updates. A Brazilian child or teenager traveling **abroad** with only one parent, alone or with third parties needs the express authorization of the absent guardian, in one of these forms:

- a form with a **notarized signature (firma reconhecida) at a notary's office (cartório)**;
- **Electronic Travel Authorization (AEV)**, made through the **e-Notariado** platform (CNJ Provision 103/2020), with a video call with the notary's office;
- an authorization already registered in the passport, requested with the PF.

**A gov.br signature is not valid.** In February 2025 the CNJ decided that an electronic signature by digital certificate or gov.br does not replace the authenticity-based signature verification done by a notary. Carry **2 copies**, because one may be kept at inspection. When traveling with both parents, no authorization is needed, but carry a copy of the birth certificate if the surnames differ. Confirm the form and the current rules on gov.br (Federal Police) and on the e-Notariado site.

## Cash and purchases abroad

- **Cash:** anyone entering or leaving Brazil with more than **US$ 10,000 (or the equivalent in another currency)** must fill in the **e-DBV** (Electronic Traveler's Goods Declaration) of the Receita Federal. Each destination country has its own limit: confirm it.
- **Purchases on return:** the Receita Federal sets an exemption allowance for goods brought in luggage and limits by type of item. Public sources indicate US$ 1,000 by air or sea; **confirm the current amount** in the Receita Federal's Traveler's Guide before planning purchases.
- **Controlled medications:** carry a prescription, preferably with the active ingredient's name and an English translation, and a quantity compatible with the trip. Some countries restrict medicines that are common in Brazil: check with the destination's embassy.

## Quick check per traveler

For each person in the group, answer:

1. Which document is valid at the destination: RG or CIN, or a passport?
2. Do the validity and issue date meet the destination's rule?
3. Is a visa or electronic authorization needed? How long does it take to obtain?
4. Does the route include a connection in a country that requires a transit visa?
5. Is a minor traveling without both guardians?
6. Does the insurance cover the required minimum?
7. Is a vaccine (CIVP) required?
8. Was everything checked on the official site less than 30 days before departure?
