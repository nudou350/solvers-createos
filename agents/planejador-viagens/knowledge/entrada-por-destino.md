# Entry rules by destination for Brazilians

Situation as of October 2, 2026, for people who hold **Brazilian citizenship only** and travel as tourists. Entry rules change by decree, ordinance or border policy, sometimes with only a few weeks' notice. Never present anything here as a guarantee of entry or visa approval: the decision always belongs to the destination country. **Confirm on the official site** (the destination's consulate or embassy, Itamaraty Consular Portal) before buying a ticket and again close to departure. Sites that charge to "issue" official authorizations are usually expensive intermediaries: use only the official addresses.

Everything in this guide is about Brazilian passport holders; other nationalities face different rules.

## United States: B1/B2 visa and ESTA

- Brazilians need a **B1/B2 tourist and business visa**, children and babies included.
- Brazil is **not part of the Visa Waiver Program**, so the **ESTA does not work for Brazilians**. Only people who also hold a passport from a participating country (for example, a European one) can use it, traveling on that passport.
- The process involves the DS-160 form, the consular fee, biometrics at the CASV (visa application center) and an interview at the consulate. Fees changed throughout 2025; do not state an amount as certain and confirm on travel.state.gov.
- The visa gives the right to request entry; the length of stay is set by the immigration officer on arrival.

## United States: interview, wait time and connections

- Since September 2025 the interview waiver has been restricted: it no longer applies by age, so children and older people also go through the interview. It remains for few renewal cases (for example, a previous visa of the same category that expired recently, among other conditions). Confirm whether your case fits on the US consulate's site in Brazil (br.usembassy.gov).
- The **interview wait time** varies from weeks to many months depending on the city. Check the wait-time tool on travel.state.gov and **only buy tickets after the visa is approved**.
- **A connection at a US airport requires a visa**, even without leaving the international area.

## Schengen area: 90 days in 180

- Brazilians have **visa exemption** for tourism for up to **90 days in any 180-day period**, adding up all the countries in the area. The period is rolling: on each day, look back 180 days and add up the days spent in the area. There is an official calculator on the European Commission site.
- Schengen is not the same as the European Union: Ireland is in the EU and not in Schengen; Switzerland, Norway and Iceland are in Schengen and not in the EU. Bulgaria and Romania have been part of the area since 2025. Confirm the current list at europa.eu.
- Passport: valid for at least 3 months after departure and issued less than 10 years ago.
- At the border they may ask for a return ticket, proof of lodging, financial means and **travel insurance with minimum medical cover of €30,000**. The formal requirement applies to those who apply for a visa, but treat insurance as mandatory.

## Europe: EES, biometric registration at the border

- The **EES (Entry/Exit System)** records the photo and fingerprints of people entering the Schengen area without a visa and replaces the passport stamp. There is no prior application and no fee: the registration is done at the border.
- It started on **October 12, 2025** and, according to official sources, has been in **full operation since April 10, 2026**.
- In September 2026 the specialized press reported that nine countries (France, Germany, Italy, Belgium, the Netherlands, Greece, Malta, Portugal and Switzerland) began applying biometric collection flexibly, with no new deadline set. In practice, the experience varies by airport: on your first entry expect queues and more time. Confirm the situation on travel-europe.europa.eu.

## Europe: ETIAS

- The **ETIAS** is a prior electronic authorization for people traveling to the Schengen area without a visa, Brazilians included. The European Commission indicates a start in the **last quarter of 2026**, with the exact date announced months ahead; the date has been postponed several times and, as of October 2, 2026, **no exact date had been announced**.
- Once it starts: a fee of **€20** per person, valid for up to **3 years** (or until the passport expires), requested online before boarding.
- The requirement will be phased in: about 6 months of transition in which nobody is turned away for lacking an ETIAS, followed by about 6 months of tolerance for first-time entrants.
- Never say it is already mandatory or that it will not be: tell the user to check **travel-europe.europa.eu**, the official site, and to be wary of intermediaries that charge extra.

## United Kingdom and Ireland

- The United Kingdom is **not in Schengen**: the 90-in-180-days count does not apply there.
- Brazilians need the **ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation)** for a short visit, requested online or through the official app. The fee is **£20** since April 8, 2026 and the authorization allows trips of up to 6 months at a time; confirm the amount and validity on gov.uk/eta.
- Ireland has its own rules, separate from the United Kingdom's: confirm with the Irish consulate.

## Canada

- **eTA** (electronic authorization, CAD 7) for Brazilians who held a Canadian visa in the last 10 years **or** have a **valid** US non-immigrant visa, and only for those who **arrive by air**. An expired US visa does not qualify for the eTA.
- Everyone else needs a visitor visa, and anyone entering by land or sea needs the visa even with a US visa. Confirm on canada.ca.

## Mexico

- Mexico requires a visa from Brazilians. Since February 2026, those traveling for tourism or business, with no paid activity, who **arrive by air** can request the **electronic authorization (SAE)** online, without going to the consulate; it is valid for one entry.
- People with a valid visa or permanent residence in countries such as the USA, Canada, the United Kingdom, Japan or the Schengen area are exempt from the Mexican visa, according to the Mexican government's rules. Those arriving by land or sea need the visa at the consulate. Confirm requirements and the fee on gob.mx/sre or with the consulate.

## Japan, Australia and other destinations

- **Japan:** the visa exemption for Brazilian tourism was extended from September 30, 2026 to September 29, 2029, for up to 90 days, and applies to people with an **ordinary passport with an electronic chip** (a passport without a chip requires a visa). Confirm with the Japanese consulate.
- **Australia:** requires a visitor visa (subclass 600), requested online. Confirm on immi.homeaffairs.gov.au.
- **The Caribbean, Africa and Asia in general:** rules and vaccine requirements vary a lot by country. Always consult the Consular Portal and the destination's consulate; if the knowledge base does not cover the destination, tell the user so.
