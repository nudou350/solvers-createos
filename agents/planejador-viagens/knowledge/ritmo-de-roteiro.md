# Itinerary pace: how to build trips that are doable

The most common mistake in AI-generated itineraries and among first-time travelers is excess: too many cities, too many attractions, zero margin. This guide gathers the method's practical rules. They are customer-service common sense, not norms: opening hours, closing days and transport connections change, and must be **confirmed on the official site** of each attraction and each operator.

## Units of time

- **Major attraction:** takes half a day or more (3 to 5 hours). Examples: the Prado Museum, the Louvre, the Vatican, the Alhambra, the Sagrada Família with its towers, a theme park (a full day), hikes, a full-day boat trip.
- **Medium attraction:** 1 to 2 hours (an important church, a viewpoint with a queue, a market).
- **Light attraction:** up to 1 hour or with no fixed time (a square, a neighborhood to stroll, a famous café).
- **Working sightseeing day:** about 8 to 10 hours away from the hotel, including meals and transfers.

Rule: **at most 1 or 2 major attractions per day**, filling in with medium and light ones. With small children or older relatives, 1 major one a day and a free afternoon.

## Transfers

- **A travel day counts as half a day**, at most. A 1-hour flight eats 4 to 6 hours door to door (check-out, ride, early arrival, flight, baggage, ride, check-in). A 3-hour train eats about 5 hours.
- **A long international arrival day counts as almost zero.** Plan a light outing near the lodging and an early dinner. Nothing with a timed ticket.
- **Jet lag:** a difference of 4 to 5 hours or more (Europe in summer, for example, is 4 to 5 hours ahead of Brasília) drags down the first and sometimes the second day. Trips to Asia (11 to 12 hours' difference) need 2 light days.
- **Train vs. plane in Europe:** a high-speed train usually beats the plane up to 3 to 4 hours of travel, because it leaves from the center and does not require a long early arrival. Beyond that, the plane is usually better. Always tell the user to check timetables and whether the connection exists on the operator's site.

## Bases and number of cities

- **Minimum of 3 nights per base** whenever possible. Two nights only in small cities; one night only to break up a long journey.
- **Quick formula:** number of nights ÷ 3 = maximum number of bases. 15 nights → up to 5 bases, and the ideal is 4.
- **Day trips** are better than changing hotels for nearby cities (up to 1h30 of travel). Classic examples: Sintra from Lisbon; Toledo and Segovia from Madrid; Versailles from Paris; Pisa or Siena from Florence; Aveiro from Porto.
- **Multi-city flight (open jaw):** arriving in one city and returning from another avoids going back to the starting point and usually costs little more than a round trip.

## Breaks

- On trips of more than 7 days, include **1 free day every 4 or 5 days**. In practice, the free day becomes shopping, laundry, rest or that attraction that was left pending.
- With children: a free afternoon on major-attraction days, a pool or a playground.

## Geographic grouping

- Organize each day by neighborhood or area. List the attractions, picture them on the map and group the nearby ones.
- Avoid crossing the city twice in the same day.
- Put dinner near where the afternoon outing ends, not near the hotel.

## Calendar and hours

- **Closing day:** many European museums close one day a week, often Monday (in Paris, the Louvre closes on Tuesdays). Confirm on each attraction's official site.
- **Local holidays** close shops and sometimes attractions; Holy Week in Spain changes hours and fills up Seville.
- **Sundays:** shops are closed in much of Germany, Austria and smaller European towns.
- **Siesta:** in smaller towns in Spain and Italy, shops close in the early afternoon.
- **Timed-entry tickets:** the Alhambra (Granada), The Last Supper (Milan), the Borghese Gallery (Rome), the Sagrada Família (Barcelona) and the Anne Frank House (Amsterdam) usually sell out weeks or months ahead. List them as a critical booking.

## Weather plan B

- For each day of outdoor activity, give a covered alternative nearby.
- European summer: heat waves are frequent in July and August; schedule outdoor activities for early morning and late afternoon, museums or rest in the middle of the day.
- Orlando in summer: heavy, quick rain almost daily in the afternoon; parks early, pool in the afternoon, the park again in the evening.

## Itineraries with children

- Daytime flights for small children when possible; night flights for children who sleep well.
- Lodging with two rooms or an apartment: parents do not need to go to bed at 8 pm.
- One "kid" activity per day (a park, zoo, aquarium, beach) keeps the peace.
- Consider a light stroller even for children who already walk, in big cities.

## Itineraries with older relatives or reduced mobility

- Fewer stairs: historic centers such as Lisbon, Porto and medieval Italian towns have slopes and staircases. Prefer flat lodging near transport.
- Request special assistance from airlines in advance (usually 48h).
- Many museums offer priority entry and loaned wheelchairs.

## Signs of an unworkable itinerary (fix before delivering)

- More than 1 base change every 3 days.
- Three or more major attractions on the same day.
- A timed attraction on the day of international arrival.
- Journeys of more than 5 hours without the day being treated as a travel day.
- No free day on a trip of 10 days or more.
- A long outing on the last day before an international flight (arrive at the airport 3h early).

## Example: 12 days in Italy, couple, first trip

The user's original request: Rome, Florence, Venice, Milan, Cinque Terre, Naples, Amalfi Coast.
Suggested correction: Rome 4 nights (the Vatican in one day, the Colosseum and Forum on another), Florence 4 nights (day trip to Siena or Pisa), Venice 3 nights, return flight leaving from Venice (open jaw). Milan, Cinque Terre and the south are left for a next trip. Result: 2 base changes, 1 free day, 3 critical bookings (the Vatican Museums, the Uffizi Gallery and the Galleria dell'Accademia, where the David is).
