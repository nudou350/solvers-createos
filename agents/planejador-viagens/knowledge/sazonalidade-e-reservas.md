# Seasonality, booking lead times and baggage

A practical guide to choosing the right time and booking at the right moment. Weather dates are historical trends, not a forecast: tell the user to look at the weather forecast in the week of the trip. Ticket sales windows, baggage allowances and airport rules change: confirm on the official site of the attraction, the airline or the airport.

Part of this guide is written from a Brazilian traveler's point of view (Brazilian school holidays and holidays that raise prices, Brazilian destinations, departures from Brazil).

## Brazilian calendar that moves prices

- **School vacations:** January (and late December) and July. Flight and lodging prices rise for domestic destinations and for Orlando, Buenos Aires, Bariloche, Santiago.
- **Carnival:** February or March, depending on the year. A sharp rise in Salvador, Recife/Olinda, Rio and inland towns with festivities; prices of short international trips also go up.
- **Long weekends:** Holy Week, Corpus Christi, Tiradentes Day, September 7, October 12, November 2 and 15. Destinations within 3 hours' flight fill up.
- **New Year's Eve:** the week between Christmas and New Year is the most expensive of the year on Brazilian beaches.

## Domestic destinations (Brazil)

- **Northeast coast:** the rainiest season in Salvador, Maceió, Recife and João Pessoa usually runs from April to July. Fortaleza and the coast of Ceará and Rio Grande do Norte have rains concentrated in the first half of the year (February to May). September to March is usually drier and sunnier.
- **Lençóis Maranhenses:** the lagoons are usually full between June and September, after the rainy season.
- **Pantanal:** flood season from December to March (beautiful scenery, harder access); dry season from July to October, better for seeing animals.
- **Amazon:** high water (May to July) favors boat trips through the igarapés (small channels); low water (September to November) exposes river beaches. Recent extreme droughts affected navigation: confirm conditions.
- **Highlands of Rio Grande do Sul and Santa Catarina:** cold from June to August, high demand in winter and at Christmas (Gramado).
- **Chapadas (Diamantina, Veadeiros):** waterfalls are full in the rainy summer, trails are drier and safer from April to September.

## South America

- **Patagonia (El Calafate, Ushuaia, Torres del Paine):** main season from November to March. Outside it, many services close.
- **Bariloche and ski resorts:** snow generally from July to September, high season in winter and during Brazil's July school break.
- **Atacama:** pleasant almost all year; the "altiplanic winter" can bring rain and close tours in January and February.
- **Machu Picchu:** dry season from May to September (high demand); February often has the Inca Trail closed for maintenance. Tickets have a daily limit: book in advance.
- **Buenos Aires:** spring (September to November) and autumn (March to May) are the most pleasant seasons.

## Europe

- **High season:** June to August. High prices, queues, intense heat in the south (Spain, Italy, Portugal, Greece) with frequent heat waves.
- **Shoulder season (the best value for money):** April to May and September to October.
- **Low season:** November to March (except Christmas, Christmas markets and ski resorts). Short days: in northern Europe in winter it gets dark before 5 pm.
- **Events that raise prices:** Holy Week in Spain, Oktoberfest in Munich (late September and early October), big fairs and conferences.
- **August:** many residents of Paris and Italian cities take vacations; some local restaurants close.

## North America and the Caribbean

- **Orlando:** high season during American and Brazilian school vacations (July, December and January). Summer has strong heat and almost daily afternoon rain. Atlantic hurricane season runs from June to November.
- **New York:** spring and autumn are pleasant; December is expensive and crowded because of the Christmas decorations; harsh winter from January to March.
- **Caribbean:** hurricane season from June to November 30, peaking from August to October. December to April is the dry and more expensive season. During the season, recommend insurance with cancellation cover and attention to rebooking policies.
- **Cancún/Riviera Maya:** besides hurricanes, sargassum arriving on the beaches is common between April and September; it varies by year.

## Asia

- **Japan:** the cherry blossom (late March to early April, varies by region) and autumn foliage (November) are the most sought-after times. Golden Week (late April to early May) and Obon (mid-August) fill up transport and hotels. Summer is hot and humid; typhoon season runs from August to October.
- **Southeast Asia:** monsoons vary by country and coast; confirm for the specific region.

## Booking lead times (reference)

| Item | Recommended lead time |
|---|---|
| International flight | 3 to 6 months; high season, 6 to 9 months |
| Domestic flight (Brazil) | 1 to 3 months; holidays, 3 to 5 months |
| Lodging in high season | 4 to 6 months; refundable rates if there is still uncertainty |
| High-speed trains in Europe | As soon as sales open (usually 2 to 4 months ahead) |
| Attractions with quotas (Alhambra, The Last Supper, Machu Picchu) | Weeks to months; check on the official site when sales open |
| Rental car | 1 to 3 months; check whether it requires a credit card in the driver's name and an International Driving Permit (PID) |
| Travel insurance | After buying the flight |

Practical tip: book lodging with free cancellation early and keep searching. Booking sites are not the only channel: it is worth comparing with the hotel's own site.

## Baggage

- **International flights leaving Brazil:** generally include at least one 23 kg checked bag in economy, but there are basic fares without a bag on some routes and airlines. Always confirm on the fare you bought.
- **Domestic flights in Brazil:** many basic fares include only carry-on baggage (10 kg in general, with maximum dimensions) and a personal item.
- **Low-cost airlines in Europe:** the cheapest fare usually includes only a small item under the seat. A larger carry-on and checked baggage are charged separately, and the price rises at the airport.
- **Liquids in carry-on:** the general rule is containers of up to 100 ml in a clear bag, with gradual changes at airports with new scanners. Confirm with the airport.
- **Lithium batteries and power banks:** carry-on only; several airlines have begun restricting their use on board.

## Plugs and voltage

- Continental Europe: type C/F plug, 230V. United Kingdom: type G. USA and Canada: type A/B, 120V. Argentina: type I. Recommend a universal adapter and checking that chargers are dual-voltage (most phone and laptop chargers are).
