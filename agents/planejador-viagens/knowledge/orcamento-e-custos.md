# Trip budget: split, hidden costs and benchmarks

This guide helps build realistic budgets for Brazilian travelers. The percentages and daily-spend ranges are **planning estimates** from the specialist's method, with no official source: they vary with exchange rates, season and style, and must never be presented as a guaranteed price. The IOF rate, on the other hand, comes from a decree and changes: always ask the user to confirm the current rate. IOF (Imposto sobre Operações Financeiras) is Brazil's tax on financial transactions, including card spending and currency purchases abroad, so this guide assumes the traveler pays from Brazil in reais.

## Split by category: international, comfort profile

The specialist's split tool uses ranges like these; adjust them to the specific case.

| Category | Range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Flights | 30-45% | Weighs more on short trips and long distances |
| Lodging | 25-35% | Expensive cities (London, Paris, New York, Zurich) push it up |
| Food | 12-20% | Included breakfast cuts 3 to 5 points |
| Activities and tickets | 8-15% | Theme parks: 20% or more |
| Local transport | 4-8% | High-speed trains and rental cars raise it to 10% |
| Insurance | 2-4% | More expensive for older travelers and for the USA |
| Emergency reserve | 10% | Never below 10% |

## Split by category: domestic, comfort profile

| Category | Range |
|---|---|
| Flights | 20-35% |
| Lodging | 30-40% |
| Food | 15-20% |
| Activities | 10-15% |
| Local transport | 5-10% |
| Emergency reserve | 10% |

Adjustments by profile: on **budget**, hostel, simple guesthouse or shared apartment lodging, food from the market and a kitchen, and free activities; flights weigh more as a percentage because the rest shrinks. On **luxury**, lodging rises to 35-45% and flights may include business class, which usually costs 3 to 5 times economy on international routes.

## Hidden costs: IOF

- IOF applies to purchases by credit, debit and prepaid card abroad, prepaid card top-ups, buying cash currency and transfers to one's own accounts abroad.
- Decrees 12,466/2025 and 12,499/2025 revoked the gradual-reduction schedule and set **3.5%** for these individual-person operations; Brazil's Supreme Court (STF) reinstated Decree 12,499 on July 16, 2025. Sources checked on October 2, 2026 indicate the rate remains 3.5% in 2026.
- Rates change by decree: cite 3.5% as the value the knowledge base knows and always ask the user to **confirm the current rate** on the Receita Federal site or with the bank. To estimate, set aside 3.5% on spending abroad and adjust later.

## Hidden costs: exchange rate and other expenses

- **Exchange spread:** the difference between the commercial exchange rate and the one the bank uses. On traditional credit cards it usually runs between 4% and 6%; on global accounts and fintech cards it is usually lower. Confirm with the issuer, including whether the purchase-day rate or the bill-payment-day rate applies.
- **Rule of thumb to estimate in reais:** the day's commercial exchange rate plus 8% to 10% (covers spread and IOF).
- **Tourist tax per night:** common in Lisbon, Porto, Barcelona, Rome, Paris, Amsterdam and other cities; it runs from about €1 to more than €10 per person per night, usually paid at the hotel and outside the booking site's price.
- **USA:** a daily resort fee at many hotels, sales tax outside the sticker price (usually 6% to 10%) and tips of 18% to 20% at restaurants with table service. In Europe, tipping is usually rounding up or 5% to 10%, with no obligation.
- **Flights and logistics:** checked baggage on basic fares, seat selection, airport transfer (it can cost a night's stay in Paris or New York), eSIM or local SIM card (carrier roaming is usually more expensive) and forgotten items, which cost more abroad.

## Reference daily spend per person at the destination

Broad ranges, only to signal whether the budget is plausible, excluding lodging and flights. These are planning estimates: confirm with current prices.

- **South America (Buenos Aires, Santiago, Lima):** budget US$ 35-60; comfort US$ 60-120.
- **Portugal and Spain:** budget €40-60; comfort €70-120.
- **France, Italy, Netherlands:** budget €50-70; comfort €90-150.
- **Switzerland, Nordic countries, London:** budget €70-100; comfort €130-200.
- **USA (New York, Orlando without tickets):** budget US$ 60-90; comfort US$ 110-180.
- **Brazilian Northeast:** budget R$ 120-200; comfort R$ 250-450.

If the calculated daily amount falls below the budget floor, say so clearly and offer options.

## How to make the budget workable

1. **Change the date:** low and shoulder season can cut lodging by 20% to 40%.
2. **Fewer cities:** each base change costs transport, fees and time.
3. **Apartment with a kitchen:** cuts food costs, especially for families.
4. **A neighborhood well served by the metro**, outside the historic center.
5. **Transport and attraction passes:** only worth it if the itinerary uses enough attractions; do the math first.
6. **Miles:** many Brazilians have idle points; ask.
7. **Connecting flights** instead of direct ones, weighing the tiredness.

## Ways to pay abroad

- **Global account or prepaid card in foreign currency:** lets you buy the currency gradually, locking in the rate, usually with a lower spread; check the current IOF for this type of operation.
- **Credit card:** practical and widely accepted; serves as a backup. Check the limit and the bank's rules for use abroad.
- **Cash:** a small amount for taxis, tips and markets. In some destinations (Argentina, for example) the most advantageous method changes often: research close to the date.
- **Neutral recommendation:** combine two methods (global account and credit card) plus a cash reserve, and never name a brand as the best. Buying currency in installments over the 3 to 6 months before reduces the risk of a one-off spike.

## Emergency reserve and a short example

The minimum reserve is **10% of the total**, kept separate and accessible (another card, another account). It covers rebooking a missed flight, an extra night, a taxi on a strike day and medication. Travel insurance does not cover everything and often reimburses afterwards; the reserve is immediate cash.

Example: a couple, 12 days in Portugal, comfort profile, R$ 30,000 with flights. Flights R$ 10,200 (34%), lodging R$ 8,400 (28%), food R$ 4,200 (14%), activities R$ 2,400 (8%), local transport R$ 1,200 (4%), insurance R$ 600 (2%), reserve R$ 3,000 (10%). Daily amount at the destination: R$ 7,800 ÷ 12 ÷ 2 = R$ 325 per person, in the range of €50 to €55 after the exchange rate with margin, plausible for a profile between budget and comfort in Portugal.
