# Travel insurance, vaccines and health

This material is planning guidance, not medical advice or an insurance purchase: health decisions (vaccines, medications, a chronic condition) belong to a doctor, and each country's health requirements change, so **confirm on the Anvisa site and with the destination's consulate**. Anvisa is Brazil's health regulator and SUSEP is Brazil's insurance regulator; the vaccine-certificate and insurer-registration parts below follow Brazilian procedures.

## Why insurance matters

Medical care abroad can be very expensive. In the USA, an emergency room visit with simple tests usually costs several thousand dollars, and a short hospital stay can reach tens or hundreds of thousands. In Europe the amounts are lower, but still high for tourists. Without insurance, travelers may have to pay out of pocket or go without elective care.

## Recommended minimum cover (medical and hospital expenses)

| Destination | Recommended minimum | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Schengen area | €30,000 | Formal requirement for a visa; treat it as mandatory for visa-exempt travelers too |
| Rest of Europe, United Kingdom | €30,000 to €60,000 | |
| USA and Canada | US$ 100,000 to US$ 150,000 | Very high medical costs |
| South America | US$ 30,000 | Some countries require insurance for tourists; confirm |
| Asia, Oceania | US$ 60,000 to US$ 100,000 | |
| Cruises | Same as the most expensive destination on the route + cover for onboard care | |

These values are planning recommendations, not official rules (except the Schengen minimum for visas). Confirm the country's requirements on the official site.

## What to check in the policy

1. **Medical and hospital expenses** at an amount suited to the destination.
2. **Pre-existing conditions:** many policies only cover the initial emergency care of a pre-existing condition, or nothing. For people with hypertension, diabetes, heart disease or another chronic condition, look for explicit cover.
3. **Age:** above 70 or 75, many plans reduce cover or raise the price. Read the table.
4. **Pregnant travelers:** check the gestational week covered (many plans cap it at 24-28 weeks) and the airlines' rules for flying.
5. **Sports and adventure activities** (skiing, diving, high-altitude hiking): usually require additional cover.
6. **Medical evacuation and repatriation** and **repatriation of remains**.
7. **Lost baggage** (compensation per item) and **baggage delay**.
8. **Trip cancellation or interruption**, useful when there are many non-refundable expenses.
9. **Emergency dental care** and medications.
10. **A 24h support line** in Portuguese, with a phone number that works at the destination and an app or WhatsApp option.
11. **The insurer's SUSEP registration** (check on the SUSEP site).

## Credit card insurance

- Some premium cards offer free travel insurance when the ticket is paid for with the card.
- Common conditions: pay for the ticket (or part of it) with the card, issue the **certificate** before the trip on the card network's site, cover for the cardholder and sometimes dependents.
- Cautions: check the medical cover amount, whether it meets the Schengen minimum, whether it covers pre-existing conditions, whether care is by reimbursement (you pay and get paid back later) or direct, and whether the certificate is accepted by immigration. Tell the user to read the conditions on the card network's site.

## Vaccines and the CIVP

- **Yellow fever:** some countries require travelers coming from Brazil to hold the **International Certificate of Vaccination or Prophylaxis (CIVP)**. The requirement is common in African countries and in some in Central and South America, and sometimes applies only to those visiting certain regions (jungle areas, national parks). The list changes: **confirm on the Anvisa site** and the WHO's before the trip.
- The vaccine must be taken at least **10 days before** departure for the certificate to be valid. A single dose is usually considered valid for life under the international rule, but confirm the destination country's requirements.
- The CIVP can be issued digitally through the official SUS (Brazil's public health system) app or site from the vaccination record, or in person at Anvisa-accredited centers. Keep a printed copy.
- There are contraindications (some immunity conditions, very advanced or very young age, pregnancy): in these cases a doctor can issue an exemption certificate. Tell the user to see a doctor; never decide for them.
- Other vaccines recommended depending on the destination (hepatitis, typhoid fever, rabies, up-to-date measles): suggest a visit to a travel medicine service 4 to 8 weeks ahead.

## Medications

- Carry regular medications in **carry-on baggage**, in an amount for the whole trip plus a margin of a few days.
- Keep the original packaging and carry a **prescription**, preferably with the active ingredient's name and an English translation.
- Controlled medications (psychotropics, some strong painkillers, some ADHD medications) may be banned or require prior authorization in certain countries (Japan and the United Arab Emirates are well-known examples of strict rules). Tell the user to check with the destination's embassy.
- Basic kit: pain reliever/fever reducer, antihistamine, motion-sickness remedy, plasters, sunscreen, repellent.

## Health while traveling

- **Altitude:** Cusco, La Paz and Atacama are above 2,400 meters. Plan 1 to 2 light days to acclimatize, hydration and little alcohol at the start. People with a heart or respiratory condition should talk to a doctor.
- **Thrombosis on long flights:** stand up and walk every 2 hours, stay hydrated, compression socks for those with a risk factor (with medical guidance).
- **Heat:** heat waves in Europe in summer and in the Middle East; schedule activities for the morning and late afternoon.
- **Water:** in many destinations tap water is drinkable (much of western Europe, the USA, Canada), in others it is not; inform and suggest confirming.

## What to do in an emergency

1. Call the **insurer's hotline** first (saved on the phone and on paper). Many plans only reimburse if care was authorized by the hotline, except in a life-threatening situation.
2. In a life-threatening situation, go to the emergency room immediately and notify the insurer afterwards.
3. Keep all receipts, medical reports and prescriptions.
4. In case of theft or loss of documents: a police report with the local police and contact with the Brazilian consulate.
5. Emergency number: 112 works across the whole European Union; 911 in the USA, Canada and Mexico.
