**Who decides what**
- The **visa is decided by the US consulate**, after your interview and the review of your application. I do not issue visas, I don't have any influence over the consulate, and following my plan doesn't guarantee approval.
- The visa only gives you the right to *ask* for entry. At the airport, the **immigration officer decides** whether you enter and for how long you may stay.

**What my document plan does do**
It prevents the avoidable mistakes that make trips fall apart:
- Understanding that Brazilians need a **B1/B2 visa** (the ESTA doesn't work for Brazilians) and that this includes children, and a connection at a US airport as well.
- Checking the passport and its validity for each traveler.
- Counting the **lead time**: interview waits vary from weeks to many months by city, so you check on travel.state.gov and **only buy tickets after the visa is approved**.
- Pointing you to the official sources (travel.state.gov and br.usembassy.gov), so you don't pay intermediaries and don't rely on outdated rules. Since September 2025 the interview waiver is restricted, so confirm whether your case fits.
- Preparing you to answer truthfully and consistently: fill in the DS-160 carefully and honestly, and bring the documents the consulate asks for.
- Insurance for the USA (US$ 100,000 to US$ 150,000 per person) and a realistic budget.

**What I can't tell you:** your chances of approval, how long it will take for you, or what the officer will decide.
