I'd change that plan: **not a good idea, and it is one of the most common first-day mistakes.**

**Why it doesn't work**
- You land at 7 am after 11 hours in the air. Then come immigration (on a first entry into Europe you also go through the EES biometric registration, with queues that vary by airport), baggage, the ride to the hotel, and usually a wait, because check-in is typically in the afternoon.
- Paris is 4 to 5 hours ahead of Brasília (depending on daylight saving time), so your body will think it is very early. Jet lag hits the first day and sometimes the second.
- The **Louvre is a major attraction** (3 to 5 hours) and the Eiffel Tower is another. Two major attractions on the day you arrive, both with timed entry in practice, break the rule of at most 1 or 2 per day, and an international arrival day should count as almost zero.
- If something runs late (a delayed bag, a long queue), you lose a non-refundable timed ticket.

**What I suggest instead**
- **Day 1 (arrival):** drop your bags (ask the hotel if they can keep them), a light walk near the hotel or along the Seine, an early lunch, a short nap if you can (not a long one), and an early dinner. Nothing with a timed ticket.
- **Day 2:** the **Louvre in the morning** (book your timed ticket online in advance and note that the Louvre closes on **Tuesdays**: confirm days and hours on the official site). Light afternoon: a neighborhood stroll or a viewpoint.
- **Day 3 or the evening of Day 2:** the **Eiffel Tower**, with a booked time slot. Going at sunset or in the evening makes a nice moment. If you only want the view, the Trocadéro across the river has a free view.
- Keep a covered plan B for rainy days (a museum or a covered passage).

**Documents (always first):** passport valid for 3 months after you leave Schengen and issued less than 10 years ago; visa exemption up to 90 days in any 180; **ETIAS**: expected in the last quarter of 2026, no exact date as of October 2, 2026 (check travel-europe.europa.eu); travel insurance with at least €30,000 in medical cover.

**To confirm on an official site:** opening hours and closing days of the Louvre and the Eiffel Tower, timed-ticket sales windows, and EES/ETIAS.
