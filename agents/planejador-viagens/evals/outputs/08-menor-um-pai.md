Yes, there are a few things besides the passport. Here is the list for you and your daughter (documents first, as always).

**1. Her own passport (and yours)**
- A child needs their own passport for Mexico (Mercosur ID shortcuts don't apply there). Validity: a good rule of thumb is at least 6 months on the return date. Passports for children are valid for less than 10 years, depending on age.
- If she doesn't have one yet: the process starts on gov.br with the Federal Police (form, GRU payment, then the in-person appointment). Start at least **60 days** before the trip, 90 in busy seasons (before July and before December).

**2. Mexico entry authorization**
- Mexico requires a visa from Brazilians. Since February 2026, those traveling for tourism who **arrive by air** can request the **electronic authorization (SAE)** online, without going to the consulate; it is valid for one entry. Each traveler, including the child, needs their own.
- People with a valid visa or permanent residence in the USA, Canada, the United Kingdom, Japan or the Schengen area are exempt. Confirm requirements and the fee on **gob.mx/sre** or with the Mexican consulate.

**3. Travel authorization from her father (the important one)**
- Under CNJ rules (Resolution 131/2011), a Brazilian child traveling abroad with **only one parent** needs the **express authorization of the absent parent**, in one of these forms:
  - a form with a **notarized signature (firma reconhecida) at a notary's office (cartório)**; or
  - the **Electronic Travel Authorization (AEV)**, done on **e-Notariado** with a video call with the notary's office; or
  - an authorization registered in her passport, requested with the Federal Police.
- **A gov.br signature is not valid.** Carry **2 copies**, because one may be kept at inspection.
- Also carry her **birth certificate** showing you as her mother, especially if surnames differ.
- If her father is deceased, or there is a court decision about custody, the documents are different: confirm with the Federal Police on gov.br or at a notary's office.

**4. Health and insurance**
- Travel insurance with decent medical and hospital cover for both of you. Buy it after the flight. Check whether cover includes children and 24h support in Portuguese.
- Emergency number in Mexico: 911.

**5. When you go matters**
- Cancún: hurricane season runs from June to November (peak from August to October), and sargassum on the beaches is common between April and September. December to April is the dry, more expensive season. Brazilian school vacations (January and July) raise prices.

**To confirm on an official site:** the minor authorization (gov.br/pf and the e-Notariado site), the SAE (gob.mx/sre), and passport process (gov.br/pf).
