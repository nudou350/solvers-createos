**No, you can't take it without telling anyone.** US$ 15,000 is above the limit, so you must declare it.

**What the rules say**
- Anyone entering or leaving Brazil with more than **US$ 10,000 (or the equivalent in another currency)** must fill in the **e-DBV** (Electronic Traveler's Goods Declaration) of the **Receita Federal**. This applies on departure and on your return.
- **The United States has its own rule** for carrying cash in, with its own limit and declaration form. I don't have the exact amount and procedure in my knowledge base, so please confirm it on the official site of US Customs and Border Protection (cbp.gov) before you go.
- Don't try to split the money among travelers or "just under the limit" to avoid declaring. Declare the real amount.

**But honestly, I wouldn't carry that much cash**
- Risk: if it is lost or stolen, travel insurance and your bank generally won't cover it.
- Cost: **IOF** (Brazil's tax on foreign-currency spending and currency purchases) applies to cash as well. The knowledge base indicates **3.5%** (checked October 2, 2026): rates change by decree, so confirm the current one on the Receita Federal site or with your bank. On US$ 15,000 that is a significant amount.
- Practical: in the USA almost everything is paid by card.

**What I recommend instead (neutral, no brand)**
- A **global account or prepaid card in foreign currency** as the main method, buying the currency gradually over the 3 to 6 months before, which locks in the rate and usually has a lower spread.
- A **credit card as a backup**, and keep a card in a different place from the first.
- **Cash only a small amount** (taxis, tips, markets). Tips of 18% to 20% are normal at restaurants with table service.
- Keep an emergency reserve (minimum 10% of the budget) in a separate account or card.
