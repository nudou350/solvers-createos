**No, I wouldn't pay that site.** Here is why.

**The ETIAS doesn't exist as something you can get yet.** The ETIAS is the prior electronic authorization for travelers who don't need a visa for the Schengen area, Brazilians included. The European Commission indicates a start in the **last quarter of 2026**, but it has been postponed several times, and as of **October 2, 2026 no exact date had been announced**. I can't tell you it is mandatory for November, and I can't say it will not be: the start will be phased, with about 6 months in which nobody is turned away for lacking it, followed by about 6 months of tolerance for first-time entrants.

**What it will cost officially:** **€20 per person**, valid for up to 3 years (or until the passport expires), requested online before boarding. R$ 400 is well above that, and a site that charges to "issue" an official authorization is an intermediary you don't need. Be wary: some of these sites charge for something that is not yet required, or add fees on top of the official one.

**What to do**
1. **Don't pay now.** Use only the official site: **travel-europe.europa.eu**. Check it again when you buy your tickets and 2 to 4 weeks before leaving.
2. Make sure of the things that are required today for Italy in November:
   - Passport valid for at least **3 months after your departure** from the Schengen area and **issued less than 10 years ago** (count from your departure, not arrival).
   - No visa for tourism: up to 90 days in any 180.
   - **EES**: photo and fingerprints at the border on your first entry (no application, no fee). Expect queues; it varies by airport.
   - Travel insurance with at least **€30,000** in medical cover; carry a printed policy.
3. If you have already given your data or paid any site, contact your bank to ask about a refund, and avoid sharing more personal information.

**To confirm on an official site:** ETIAS and EES (travel-europe.europa.eu), entry rules (Itamaraty Consular Portal, gov.br/mre).
