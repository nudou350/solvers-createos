Quick and important correction first: Brazilians **cannot use the ESTA**. Brazil is not part of the US Visa Waiver Program, so the ESTA does not work for you or your husband. The only exception is someone who also holds a passport from a participating country (for example a European one) and travels on that passport.

What you each need is a **B1/B2 tourist visa** in your passport. Here is what that means in practice:

**Documents (for each of you)**
- Passport valid for the trip. A good rule of thumb is at least 6 months of validity on the return date. Tell me the month/year it expires (never the number).
- The visa process: DS-160 form, consular fee, biometrics at the visa application center (CASV) and an interview at the consulate. Fees changed during 2025, so I won't quote an amount: check travel.state.gov.
- Since September 2025 the interview waiver is much more restricted. It only remains for a few renewal cases (for example, a previous visa of the same category that expired recently). If you already had a US visa, confirm on br.usembassy.gov whether your case fits.
- The visa gives you the right to *ask* for entry; the officer at the airport decides whether you enter and for how long.

**Lead time: this is the point that worries me**
December is only about 2 months away. Interview wait times vary from weeks to many months depending on the city. Please check the current wait time for your city on **travel.state.gov** before anything else, and **do not buy flights or hotels until the visa is approved**. If the wait does not fit December, the honest options are: move the dates (for example, to next year) or choose a destination without a visa.

**Other things to know for New York in December**
- December is expensive and crowded because of the Christmas decorations. If your dates are flexible, early December or January to March is cheaper (but January to March is a harsh winter).
- Travel insurance: for the USA I recommend medical cover of US$100,000 to US$150,000 per person, because medical costs there are very high.
- If your route includes a connection at a US airport (for example, if you were thinking of going via another country), that also requires the visa.

**To confirm on an official site:** visa process, fees and wait times at travel.state.gov and br.usembassy.gov (knowledge base checked on October 2, 2026; these rules change often).

To plan the rest, tell me:
1. Do you or your husband already have a valid US visa, or another citizenship?
2. Until when are both passports valid (month and year)?
3. Dates (or a window) and how many days?
4. Approximate budget in reais, and whether it includes flights?
5. Departure city, and is anything already booked?
6. Do you prefer a relaxed, moderate or intense pace?
