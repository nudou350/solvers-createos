## Every ad

- [ ] The piece identifies itself as advertising (CDC art. 36; CONAR Code art. 28).
- [ ] Each objective claim has proof at hand: a report, an auditable number, an authorized testimonial (CDC art. 38).
- [ ] No superlative or comparison without a source ("the best", "no. 1", "cheapest").
- [ ] No promise of results ("guaranteed", "end", "lose weight").
- [ ] Price, installments (with interest and total, if any) and offer validity are clear (CDC arts. 30, 31 and 52).
- [ ] Essential condition kept out of the "see more" and the footer: shipping, deadline, restriction, "free" with a condition.
- [ ] Urgency and scarcity are real and have a date or quantity.
- [ ] Nothing that exploits fear or superstition, and nothing aimed at children (CDC art. 37, §2).

## If there is an influencer or affiliate

- [ ] Disclosure on the first line or in the native "Paid partnership" label, not depending on "see more".
- [ ] Coupon and commission link treated as advertising.
- [ ] Brand and influencer know what the brand can prove.

## If the sector is regulated

- [ ] Professional identified with name and registration number (CRM and RQE, CREFITO, CRO, OAB).
- [ ] No price, discount or free offer where the council prohibits it (law, dentistry; confirm with the regional council).
- [ ] Before and after only where the rule allows it, with a consent form and without identifying the patient.
- [ ] Supplement or cosmetic with no claim to treat, cure or prevent disease; the supplement claim is on Anvisa's current list.
- [ ] Fixed-odds betting: do not publish (MP 1.394/2026, while in force).

## If it is email, SMS or WhatsApp

- [ ] The list is made of customers or people who gave consent; it is not bought, rented or scraped (LGPD).
- [ ] Sender identified at the start of the message.
- [ ] Free and visible unsubscribe ("Reply STOP" or a link).
- [ ] The footer says why the person is receiving it.
- [ ] No sensitive data (health, religion etc.) used in the message or in the segmentation.
