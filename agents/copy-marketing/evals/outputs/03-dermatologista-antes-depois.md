I can help, but not with the post exactly as described. A physician's advertising follows CFM Resolution 2.336/2023, and it draws a line between an **educational** before-and-after and one made **to attract patients**. This review is advisory and applies Brazilian rules only.

## What the rule allows and what it does not

- **Before and after: only for an educational purpose.** That means an educational text alongside, the indications and the factors that may influence the result, no image manipulation and **no way to identify the patient**. A post whose goal is "to attract new patients" with a transformation photo is the format the rule warns against.
- **Price: allowed**, with payment methods and promotions, if clear and without sensationalism.
- **Prohibited:** guaranteeing, promising or hinting at good results; sensationalism; superlatives; comparison with colleagues. Melasma often relapses and responds differently from patient to patient, so "melasma gone" or "clear skin" captions are out.
- **Identification (mandatory):** your name, the word "médica" or "médico", CRM with the state and, because dermatology is a specialty, the RQE. Only advertise a specialty you have registered.
- The patient's **written consent** for the use of the images is needed (confirm the form with your regional council). Also check Meta's own policies on health and before-and-after images, which can be stricter than the law.
