I can't write this message, and I'd advise against sending it in any form. This review is advisory and applies Brazilian rules only; for the health-data and professional-confidentiality side, please confirm with a lawyer and your professional council.

## Why not

1. **Sensitive personal data.** A diagnosis is health data, which the LGPD (Law 13.709/2018, art. 11) treats as sensitive. Marketing messages must never include sensitive data nor infer it in segmentation without a specific legal basis. Using a patient list to pick "the diabetic ones" for a sales message is exactly that. "We know you have diabetes" also exposes the diagnosis to anyone who sees the phone screen.
2. **Therapeutic claim.** "Controls blood sugar" attributes treatment to a supplement. Supplement copy cannot claim treatment, cure or control of disease (Anvisa RDC 243/2018 and IN 28/2018); it would also fail CDC art. 38 (burden of proof on the advertiser) and, aimed at people with a diagnosis, risks being abusive by exploiting a vulnerable situation (CDC art. 37).
3. **Legal basis for the list.** Patients gave you their data for care, not to be targeted for a product sale. Legitimate interest covers similar products to what a current customer already bought, not a different product based on their diagnosis, and consent for this purpose is not on record.

## What I can write instead

- A **general message to patients who opted in to receive news from the clinic**, with no diagnosis or segmentation by health condition: new hours, services, educational content for everyone.
- **Supplement copy with no therapeutic claim** (routine, directions for use, what is on the label, within an Anvisa-authorized claim only), for people who consented to receive offers, not segmented by diagnosis.
- An **opt-in form** for the clinic's marketing: a separate, clear consent for offers and a visible unsubscribe, so future messages have a proper legal basis.

If you want one of these, tell me: how patients joined the list and whether they agreed to receive offers; the supplement's name, price and the authorized claim on its label (if any); the clinic's professional identification (name and council registration of the responsible professional); and the tone. Every message will carry the sender at the start and a way to stop receiving it (LGPD arts. 8 §5 and 9; the number of messages and timing respected).
