# LGPD in email marketing, SMS and WhatsApp

Knowledge checked on October 2, 2026: the General Data Protection Law (LGPD, Law no. 13.709/2018, Brazil's data protection law) and guidance from the ANPD (Brazil's data protection authority). Confirm the rule in force and the latest ANPD guidance before large campaigns. This note covers Brazilian law; campaigns to recipients in other countries may fall under other rules (GDPR, CAN-SPAM, etc.) that this package does not cover.

## Why email copy needs to know the LGPD

Email addresses, phone numbers and names are **personal data**. Sending a campaign is **data processing** and requires a **legal basis**. The copy is directly affected: the message must identify who is sending, explain why the person is receiving it and offer an easy unsubscribe. A campaign that is technically well written for an irregular list exposes the user to ANPD sanctions, complaints to Procon (Brazil's consumer protection agency) and blocking by providers.

## Possible legal bases for marketing

- **Consent (art. 7, I; art. 8)**: a free, informed and unambiguous statement, for a specific purpose. A pre-ticked box, consent "buried" in the terms of use or generic consent ("for various purposes") does not work. Consent can be **revoked at any time**, through a **free and easy** procedure (art. 8, §5).
- **Legitimate interest (art. 7, IX; art. 10)**: possible for informing **current customers** about products similar to what they already bought, respecting the data subject's legitimate expectations, with transparency and a documented **balancing test**. It does not cover lists of strangers.
- The burden of showing that consent was properly obtained is on the controller (art. 8, §2).

## Purchased or scraped list: do not use

- A list that was bought, rented or collected from websites ("scraping") **has no legal basis** for marketing by whoever bought it: the data subjects never consented to that sender nor have any relationship with it.
- Besides the legal risk, it wrecks deliverability (spam reports, spam traps, domain blocking).
- Solver conduct: **do not write the campaign for a purchased list**. Explain the reason (LGPD, legal basis) and propose the alternative: a digital lead magnet with opt-in (free material, assessment, coupon in exchange for signing up), targeted ads, a partnership with someone who has their own consented base, LinkedIn or sales visits for B2B.

## Data subject rights that show up in the copy

- **Transparency (art. 9)**: state who the controller is, the purpose and how to get in touch. In email: company name, address or CNPJ (Brazilian company tax ID) and a contact channel in the footer.
- **Revocation and objection (arts. 8, §5 and 18)**: a visible unsubscribe link that works in **one click or a few steps**, with no login and no need to give a reason.
- **Access, correction and deletion (art. 18)**: a channel for the data subject to ask (the data protection officer's (DPO) email or a form).
- Useful footer sentence: "You are receiving this email because you signed up at [source] on [date or period]. [Unsubscribe] | [Privacy policy]".

## Copy rules for email marketing

1. **Recognizable sender**: the brand's name and domain; no generic "Support" that misleads.
2. **Truthful subject line**: do not fake a reply ("RE:"), an invoice, an order tracking notice or a personal message. A misleading subject line is misleading advertising (CDC art. 37).
3. **Preheader** complementing the subject, within 40 to 90 characters.
4. **Clear advertising disclosure** if the email is promotional (CDC art. 36).
5. **Unsubscribe** in the footer, visible, not tiny gray hidden text.
6. **Frequency**: say at sign-up how many emails the person will receive, and keep to it.

## SMS and WhatsApp

- Same legal bases: **opt-in** or a customer relationship with documented legitimate interest.
- Identify the sender at the **start** of the message ("Pizzaria Bella:").
- Offer a way to stop: "Reply STOP to opt out". Honor it immediately.
- WhatsApp Business: Meta requires opt-in and approved templates for company-initiated messages; numbers that receive many reports have sending limited or blocked.
- Timing: avoid late night and early Sunday; besides being bad for conversion, it generates reports.
- Never include sensitive data (health, religion, sexual orientation etc.) in a marketing message, nor infer it in segmentation without a specific legal basis (art. 11).

## LGPD checklist for the piece

- [ ] Legal basis for the list identified (consent, or customer with legitimate interest).
- [ ] The list is not bought, rented or scraped.
- [ ] Sender and company identified.
- [ ] Free, easy and visible unsubscribe.
- [ ] Reason for receiving explained in the footer.
- [ ] No sensitive data used in the message.
