# Copy formulas and structure

How to turn a briefing into copy that sells without promising what it cannot deliver. Use together with the channel limits knowledge base and the legal knowledge base. This is a method, not a law: style rules have no expiry date. The legal references (CDC, CONAR, Receita Federal) are Brazilian.

## Minimum briefing before writing

Without these six items, the copy turns into a list of adjectives:

1. **Audience**: a concrete person at a concrete moment ("a salon owner who loses appointments for lack of reminders", not "women 25-45").
2. **Offer**: what it is, price, condition (installments with or without interest, shipping, deadline, validity).
3. **Main pain or desire**, in the customer's words.
4. **Objection number one**: "it's expensive", "I don't have time", "will it work for me?", "I already tried and it didn't work".
5. **Available proof**: verifiable numbers, real and authorized testimonials, certifications, time in business, formal guarantee.
6. **Channel and format**, with the character limit of each field.

Add the **sector**: health, law, supplements, cosmetics, betting, finance and children's products have their own rules (see the regulated sectors knowledge base). If the user does not know something, propose an assumption and mark it as an assumption.

## AIDA: pages, emails and videos

- **Attention**: a specific fact or question for the audience ("Does your cake sink in the middle?").
- **Interest**: the mechanism, the "how it works" ("the problem is usually oven temperature and batter consistency").
- **Desire**: proof and the scene after purchase (a real testimonial, a verifiable number, what changes in the routine).
- **Action**: a single CTA, with the concrete next step ("See the class schedule").

Good for pieces with room. In a short ad, AIDA becomes one sentence per letter.

## PAS: short ads and social media

- **Problem**: the concrete pain.
- **Agitation**: the real consequence, without drama ("loses 2 hours a week checking spreadsheets", not "your life is chaos").
- **Solution**: the offer as the way out, with proof.

Careful: agitation that exploits fear or superstition can make the advertising **abusive** (CDC art. 37, §2). In health, agitating a symptom to sell a treatment also violates professional codes of ethics.

## BAB and 4P: useful variations

- **BAB (Before, After, Bridge)**: how it is today, how it looks afterwards, the bridge (the product). Good for services. Do not use "before and after" with a promise of results in health and aesthetics.
- **4P (Promise, Picture, Proof, Proposal)**: a **provable** promise, a concrete scene, proof, an offer with conditions.

## 4U: headlines and subject lines

Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific. A headline with three of the four already stands out.

- "File your income tax in 40 minutes, without leaving home": useful, specific, unique for people who dread the paperwork. Urgency goes in the description and uses the **official deadline of the current year** (confirm the date with the Brazilian tax authority, Receita Federal, before publishing).
- "Pilates for the back, classes of 4 students": useful, specific, unique.
- Urgency only if it is **true** and verifiable ("enrollment until October 15", "12 seats per class"). A permanent "Last spots" is misleading advertising.

## Benefit before feature

A feature is what the product has; a benefit is what changes for the customer. Write the benefit and prove it with the feature:

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Online booking 24/7 | Your client books at midnight and you wake up to a full calendar |
| Classes of 4 students | The instructor corrects your posture in every exercise |
| Delivery in 2 business days | Arrives before Saturday's birthday |

Use the audience's language. "Innovative solutions" and "excellence in service" say nothing; "we reply on WhatsApp within 1 hour" does.

## Swap promises for verifiable facts

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "The best in town" | "Serving the Cambuí neighborhood since 2012" |
| "Guaranteed results" | "Full assessment at the first appointment" |
| "End your pain for good" | "Lower back pain treatment with a physiotherapist" |
| "Earn R$ 10k a month" | "Learn pricing with a ready-made spreadsheet" |
| "Lose 10 kg" | (no safe swap: remove the claim) |
| "Cheapest in Brazil" | "R$ 60 upfront or 3 x R$ 20 interest-free" |

Whoever advertises must prove what they claim (CDC art. 38). If there is no proof, the phrase goes.

## Variations for testing

- Deliver 3 to 5 variations and change **one axis at a time**: angle (pain x desire), proof (number x testimonial), offer (discount x bonus) or CTA.
- Name each one: "V1 pain", "V2 numeric proof", "V3 CTA book".
- State the metric: CTR for headlines and ads, open rate for subject lines, conversion rate for pages, replies for WhatsApp.
- Do not declare a winner with a few dozen clicks: wait for volume (as a rule of thumb, a few hundred clicks or ~100 conversions per variation) or use the platform's own A/B test, which calculates significance.

## CTA: one per piece

- A single next step, with a concrete verb: "Book your assessment", "See the schedule", "Request a quote on WhatsApp".
- The CTA must match what happens after the click. "Free" in the CTA and a charge on the page is a misleading offer.
- In some fields the CTA is restricted: betting advertising cannot have an immediate call to action (Portaria SPA/MF 1.231/2024) and law firms cannot encourage litigation (Provimento OAB 205/2021).

## Quality checklist before the legal review

1. A single, clear CTA.
2. Hook within the first visible characters of the channel.
3. Benefit before feature.
4. Every claim has proof identified or was removed.
5. The audience's language, no internal jargon.
6. Each text with its character count beside it.
