# Advertising in Brazil: the CDC and the CONAR Code

Knowledge checked on October 2, 2026. It guides the writing; it does not replace legal advice. Rules change: confirm the one in force before publishing, especially for large campaigns or regulated sectors. This note covers Brazilian law only: the CDC is Brazil's Consumer Defense Code (Law 8.078/1990) and CONAR is the Brazilian advertising self-regulation council.

## Who answers for the ad

- The **advertiser** (the sponsor) answers for the truthfulness of what is claimed. The agency, copywriter and influencer can also be held liable, depending on the case.
- The AI that writes the copy does not transfer responsibility: the advertiser is the one who publishes. That is why every claim needs proof that **the user has at hand**.
- Four layers add up: **law** (CDC, LGPD, sector laws), **councils and agencies** (CFM, OAB, Anvisa etc.), **self-regulation** (CONAR) and **platform policies** (Google, Meta), which can be stricter.

## CDC art. 36: advertising must be identifiable

Consumer Defense Code (Law no. 8.078/1990), art. 36: advertising must be conveyed in such a way that the consumer **easily and immediately** identifies it as such.

- It bans disguised advertising: a paid post that looks like spontaneous opinion, a sponsored "news story" with no notice, a testimonial from a business partner with no mention of the partnership.
- Sole paragraph: the supplier must keep in its possession the **factual, technical and scientific data** that support the message.
- In practice: paid influencer content carries a clear disclosure; an advertorial carries a visible "advertising" or "sponsored content" label.

## CDC art. 37: misleading and abusive advertising

Art. 37 prohibits all misleading or abusive advertising.

- **Misleading (§1)**: information that is totally or partly false, or that, even **by omission**, misleads the consumer about nature, characteristics, quality, quantity, properties, origin, price or other data.
- **By omission (§3)**: failing to state an **essential fact**. Examples: the price applies only to upfront payment, the "free" requires a subscription, the offer is limited to one city.
- **Abusive (§2)**, among others: discriminatory, inciting violence, **exploiting fear or superstition**, taking advantage of the lack of judgment and experience of **children**, or inducing behavior harmful to health or safety.

## Examples of typical copy risk

| Phrase | Problem |
|---|---|
| "Last spots!" (always running) | False urgency: misleading |
| "From R$ 99" with no item at R$ 99 | Misleading price |
| "Free shipping*" with the condition only in the footer | Omission of an essential fact |
| "If you don't do this, your child will suffer" | Exploiting fear: abusive |
| "Approved by dermatologists" with no study | Claim without proof (art. 38) |

## CDC art. 38: whoever advertises must prove it

Art. 38: the **burden of proof** of the truthfulness and correctness of the advertising information or communication **rests with whoever sponsors it**.

- The consumer does not have to prove that "best in town" is false: the advertiser has to prove it is true.
- Writing rule: **every objective claim needs identifiable proof** (a survey, a report, a registration, an auditable internal number, an authorized testimonial). Without proof, rewrite it as a verifiable fact or remove it.
- Superlatives ("the best", "the fastest", "no. 1", "leader") and comparisons require an identifiable, current source.

## CDC arts. 30, 31 and 52: the offer binds and the price must be clear

- **Art. 30**: sufficiently precise information or advertising **binds** the supplier and becomes part of the contract. If the ad says "delivery in 24 h", the customer can demand it.
- **Art. 31**: the offer must carry correct, clear, precise and prominent information, in Portuguese, about characteristics, quality, quantity, price, warranty, deadlines and risks.
- **Art. 52**: in credit or financed sales, state the upfront price, interest, surcharges, number and frequency of installments and the total payable.
- **Arts. 67 and 69**: making or promoting advertising that one knows or should know is misleading or abusive, and failing to organize the data that back the advertising, are **crimes** against the consumer.

## Advertising to children

- CDC art. 37, §2: advertising that takes advantage of the lack of judgment and experience of children is abusive.
- Conanda Resolution no. 163/2014 (Conanda is Brazil's national council for children's rights) considers it abusive to target advertising at children with childlike language, characters, collectible giveaways and the like. Confirm the rule in force.
- The CONAR Code has a specific section for children and young people (art. 37).
- Writing rule: speak to the **parents or guardians**; never ask the child to convince the adult ("ask your mom").

## CONAR Code: the articles that weigh most on copy

The Brazilian Code of Advertising Self-Regulation is self-regulation: CONAR can recommend changing or suspending an ad, and its decisions are usually followed by media outlets.

- **Art. 1**: every ad must be respectful, comply with the country's laws, and be honest and truthful.
- **Art. 27**: **truthful** presentation of the product; descriptions, claims and comparisons must be provable.
- **Art. 28**: the ad must be **clearly distinguished as such**, whatever its form or medium.
- **Art. 32**: **comparative advertising** is accepted if it clarifies, compares **objective** and provable data, does not disparage the competitor and does not cause confusion between brands.
- **Art. 37**: children and young people.
- The **annexes** deal with specific categories (alcoholic drinks, food, health, vehicles, betting). For a sector with an annex, check the current text at conar.org.br.

## Checklist of risky promises

Before delivering, look for and handle:

1. **Superlatives and comparatives** with no source ("best", "biggest", "no. 1", "cheapest"): proof, or swap for a fact.
2. **Promise of results** ("guaranteed", "cure", "end", "lose X kg", "earn R$ X"): remove it, above all in health, aesthetics, income, investments and betting.
3. **Price and condition**: "from" with a real value; installments with interest and total; offer validity.
4. **Urgency and scarcity**: only if true and with a date or quantity.
5. **Testimonials**: real and authorized in writing; an atypical result calls for a caveat ("results vary").
6. **"Free"**: no catch; if it requires sign-up, subscription or purchase, say so.
7. **Advertising disclosure**: paid posts, advertorials and affiliate content identified (CDC art. 36, CONAR art. 28).
8. **Regulated sector**: health, law, supplements, cosmetics, betting, drinks, finance and children have extra rules.
9. **Personal data**: email, SMS and WhatsApp only with a legal basis and easy unsubscribe.

Always explain to the user **what was changed and why**, citing the rule.
