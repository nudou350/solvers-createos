# Health and legal advertising

Knowledge checked on October 2, 2026. These rules add to the CDC and CONAR. They come from Brazilian professional councils: CFM (physicians), COFFITO (physiotherapy and occupational therapy), CFO (dentistry) and OAB (the Brazilian Bar Association). Councils change rules often and regional branches interpret details differently: confirm the rule in force on the council's website and cite it to the user. This package does not cover other countries' professional rules.

## How to tell whether the sector is regulated

Always ask: **who signs off on the service?** A physician, dentist, physiotherapist, nutritionist, psychologist, lawyer or other professional with a council: advertising follows the profession's code of ethics, even when the ad is for the clinic or the firm.

A writing rule common to health and law: **no promise of results, guarantee, superlative or sensationalism**, and **identification of the professional** (name and council registration number).

## Medicine: CFM Resolution no. 2.336/2023

The medical advertising rule in force since March 2024; it replaced CFM Resolution 1.974/2011. Details on the CFM's medical advertising portal.

**Mandatory**
- Identification: name, the word "physician" ("médico" or "médica"), **CRM** number with the state (UF) and, if advertising a specialty, the **RQE** (specialist registration number). On social media, these details in the profile.
- Only advertise a registered specialty.

**Allowed, with conditions**
- Publishing **prices** and payment methods, and running promotions, clearly and without sensationalism.
- **Before and after** only for an **educational** purpose: educational text alongside, indications and factors that may influence the result, with no image manipulation and without identifying the patient.
- Selfies and images of the practice and equipment, without sensationalism.

**Prohibited**
- Guaranteeing, promising or hinting at good results.
- Sensationalism, superlatives ("the best", "the only one in the region"), a "miracle" or "revolutionary" technique and comparison with colleagues.

## Physiotherapy and occupational therapy: COFFITO

- **COFFITO Resolution 424/2013** is the Code of Ethics of Physiotherapy; **425/2013**, the one for Occupational Therapy. **COFFITO Resolution 532/2021** began to allow the publication of authentic images, texts and audio of procedures, by the responsible professional, with an **informed consent form (TCLE)** from the patient, the professional's name and registration number and the date of the material.
- Mandatory: name and **CREFITO** number (e.g. CREFITO-3/12345-F).
- Prohibited: promise of results ("cure", "end the pain", "100% effective"), sensationalism, unfair competition, a third party's clinical case.
- **Before and after**: the Code of Ethics has traditionally prohibited it and councils disagree on the reach of 532/2021. Safe reading: avoid the format or confirm with the regional CREFITO and use it only with consent and without identifying the patient.
- Price as a lure and a "free session": restrictive guidance in several regional CREFITOs. Confirm in your region.

## Dentistry: CFO

- **Dental Code of Ethics (CFO Resolution 118/2012)** and **CFO Resolution 196/2019**, which covers diagnostic and result images.
- CFO-196/2019 allows the **individual dentist** responsible for the case to publish **diagnosis and final result** images with a signed **TCLE**, name and **CRO number**.
- Still prohibited: this kind of post by a **clinic (legal entity)**, photos of the procedure in progress, self-promotion, sensationalism, superlatives and **guaranteed results**.
- Price, free offers and discounts as a hook remain prohibited. **CFO Resolution 271/2025** (June 18, 2025) allowed giveaways, raffles, gift cards and discount cards as a commercial practice, but did not allow advertising "20% off" as an offer. Confirm with the regional CRO.

## Law: OAB Provimento no. 205/2021

A rule of the OAB Federal Council on legal advertising and marketing, alongside the Statute and the Code of Ethics and Discipline.

- **Allowed**: informative legal content marketing, including paid boosting, with sobriety and without commercialization.
- **Art. 3, prohibited**: references to **fee amounts, payment terms, free services or discounts**; information that misleads; unproven specialty; **persuasive, self-aggrandizing or comparative** expressions; indiscriminate distribution of material.
- **Art. 4, §2**: mentioning court decisions and results obtained is prohibited ("we won 300 cases").
- **Art. 6**: **showing off** assets and **promising results** are prohibited.
- **Soliciting clients** and encouraging litigation are prohibited by the Statute and the Code of Ethics: nothing like "Fired? Sue now!".
- Safe copy: educational content ("Which payments you receive in a dismissal without cause"), the lawyer identified with the OAB number and an informative CTA ("Read the full guide").
