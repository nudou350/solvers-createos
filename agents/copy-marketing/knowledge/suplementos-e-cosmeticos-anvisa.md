# Supplements and cosmetics: Anvisa

Knowledge checked on October 2, 2026. Anvisa is Brazil's health regulatory agency. The list of authorized claims and the labeling rules are updated by Anvisa: confirm the current version on the agency's website before using a claim. This note covers Brazil only.

## Food supplements

- **RDC 243/2018**: composition, quality, safety and labeling of food supplements.
- **IN 28/2018**: lists of constituents, limits and **authorized claims**. You may only use the claim that is on the list **for that constituent**, with the wording and conditions provided. The IN is updated from time to time; check the current list.
- A supplement is not a medicine: the copy talks about **nutrition**, within the authorized claim. Example: "Calcium helps in the formation of bones", if the product meets the requirements of that claim.

## What supplement copy cannot say

- **Treatment, cure or prevention of disease**: "cures arthrosis", "fights diabetes", "prevents cancer".
- Therapeutic or medicinal action and any claim outside the list. Terms such as "slims", "burns fat" and "reduces bloating" usually have no basis in the list.
- A testimonial like "I cured my arthrosis": it is an indirect therapeutic claim, also prohibited.
- "Approved by Anvisa" as a selling point: regularization is an obligation, not a differentiator.

If the user asks for a prohibited claim, explain the reason, remove it from the piece and offer the alternative within the list (or a routine benefit, such as flavor, convenience and directions for use).

## Cosmetics

- **RDC 752/2022**: requirements for the regularization and labeling of personal hygiene products, cosmetics and perfumes. Confirm later changes.
- A cosmetic **cannot claim therapeutic action** ("treats acne", "cures dermatitis", "eliminates cellulite", "regenerates tissue"). A therapeutic claim effectively turns the product into an unregistered medicine.
- Cosmetic benefits ("moisturizes for 24 h", "reduces apparent oiliness") require **proof of efficacy** kept by the company, and CDC art. 38 reinforces the burden of proof.
- In an influencer paid post, the same applies: an honest account of use instead of "my spots disappeared in 7 days".

## Other sectors that call for caution

- **Nutrition, psychology, physical education and aesthetics**: each council (CFN, CFP, CONFEF) has its own rules. Apply "no promise of results, with registration identification" and ask the user to confirm with the council.
- **Alcoholic drinks**: Law 9.294/1996 and the CONAR Code annex (warning; do not associate with success, driving or sport; nothing aimed at minors).
- **Investments and credit**: do not promise returns; past returns do not guarantee future ones; there are rules from the CVM (Brazil's securities regulator) and the Central Bank.
- **Medicines**: advertising restricted by Anvisa; a prescription-only medicine cannot be advertised to the lay public. Refer to a regulatory specialist.
