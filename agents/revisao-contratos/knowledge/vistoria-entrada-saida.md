# Move-in and move-out inspection of a rented property

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Move-in inspection: why it is the most important document

- The tenant must return the property **in the condition in which they received it, except for normal wear and tear** (Law 8.245/1991, art. 23, III). Without a record of the initial condition, the discussion at move-out becomes one person's word against the other's.
- The landlord is required to provide, **if the tenant asks**, a **detailed description of the property's condition** at handover, listing the existing defects (art. 22, V). Always ask for it.
- The landlord is liable for **defects that predate** the lease (art. 22, IV) and must deliver the property in usable condition (art. 22, I).

## How to do the move-in inspection (step by step)

1. Do it before moving the furniture in, in daylight.
2. Go room by room: walls, ceiling, floor, doors, windows, locks, outlets, switches, taps, valves, flushes, drains, shower enclosure, closets, included appliances.
3. Test everything: open taps, flush, switch on lights, run the shower, check for leaks and mold.
4. Take **dated photos and videos**, with an overview and close-ups of the defects.
5. Describe it in writing, in objective language ("20 cm damp stain on the bathroom ceiling").
6. **Attach the report to the contract**, signed by both parties. If the agency prepared the report, check it and write down any disagreements within a few days, in writing.
7. Record water, electricity and gas readings and the number of keys.

## Move-out inspection and normal wear and tear

- Compare with the move-in report. The tenant is liable for damage caused by them, family members, visitors or agents (art. 23, V), but **not for normal wear and tear** from use (art. 23, III): paint aged by time, small marks of use, floor wear from ordinary use.
- Clauses like "return with fresh paint" are common. If the property was delivered **freshly painted** and that is in the report, the requirement tends to be accepted; if not, it is a point of **attention** and negotiation.
- Do the move-out inspection **with both parties present**, with photos, and ask for the signed key handover form. The guarantee usually lasts until the keys are actually returned (art. 39).
- If the landlord wants to inspect during the contract, they must **agree on a day and time** (art. 23, IX). A clause that allows entry at any time, without notice, is **attention/abusive**.

## Inspection for the individual landlord

- The move-in report also protects the landlord: without it, it is hard to prove that damage was caused by the tenant.
- Also record included furniture and appliances, with condition and model.
- Keep the report together with the contract and the photos in the cloud.
