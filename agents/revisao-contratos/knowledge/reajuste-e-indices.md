# Rent adjustment: annual frequency and indexes (IGP-M vs IPCA)

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Rent can only be adjusted once a year (Law 10.192/2001)

- An adjustment or inflation correction with a **period shorter than one year is void by operation of law** (Law 10.192/2001, art. 2, par. 1). Devices that produce the same effect as a more frequent adjustment are also void (art. 2, par. 3).
- Therefore, a **semiannual, quarterly or monthly** adjustment clause is void in that part: the adjustment can only be **annual**, counted from the start of the contract or from the last adjustment/revision (art. 2, par. 2).
- Under the Tenancy Law: the parties are free to agree the rent, but it is **prohibited** to set it in a **foreign currency** or tie it to the **exchange rate** or to the **minimum wage** (Law 8.245/1991, art. 17; art. 85). For residential leases, the adjustment criteria of the specific legislation apply (art. 17, sole paragraph).

## Index clause: which index to use and how to write it

- The contract should say **which index**, **the base date** and **what happens if the index is discontinued** (replacement index).
- Common indexes:
  - **IGP-M (FGV)**: heavily influenced by the exchange rate and wholesale prices; it is volatile. In 2021 it exceeded 30% over 12 months, and in other periods it was negative.
  - **IPCA (IBGE)**: the official consumer inflation index; it tends to be more stable and closer to the cost of living.
  - **IVAR (FGV)**: the residential rent variation index, created to measure actual rents.
- No index is "the right one" under the law: the choice is free, as long as it is a price index (Law 10.192/2001, art. 2; Law 8.245/1991, art. 85). For the tenant, the IPCA tends to be more predictable; for the landlord, it is worth looking at the history and the market.
- Good wording: "The rent will be adjusted every 12 months, counted from [date], by the cumulative change in the IPCA/IBGE over the period. If the index is discontinued, the official index that replaces it will be used. In case of a negative change, the rent will remain unchanged."
- Without an adjustment clause, there is no automatic adjustment: the parties can agree a new amount or index at any time (art. 18). The Civil Code's adjustment index (IPCA, art. 389, sole paragraph) applies to correct **overdue debts**, not to adjust rent.

## How to calculate the annual rent adjustment

```
new rent = current rent × (1 + cumulative change of the index over 12 months)
```

Example: rent of R$ 2,000, cumulative IPCA of 4.5% → 2,000 × 1.045 = **R$ 2,090**.
If the contract used an index that accumulated 10% in the same period → **R$ 2,200**. The annual difference would be R$ 110 × 12 = R$ 1,320.

Always use the **real cumulative index** for the period, checked on the FGV website (IGP-M, IVAR) or IBGE (IPCA), or the Central Bank's Citizen's Calculator. Never invent the percentage: if the user does not know it, run the calculation with a hypothetical rate and say it is a simulation.

## Negotiating the adjustment and the review action (arts. 18 and 19)

- The parties can, **at any time and by mutual agreement**, set a new amount or swap the index (art. 18). With the IGP-M far above inflation, it is common to negotiate a switch to the IPCA.
- Without agreement, after **3 years** of contract (or since the last agreement), the landlord **or** the tenant can ask for a **court review** to adjust the rent to the market price (art. 19).
