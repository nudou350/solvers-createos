# Improvements and the tenant's right of first refusal (arts. 27 to 36)

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Improvements (benfeitorias): necessary, useful and luxury (arts. 35 and 36)

- **Necessary** (they prevent the property from deteriorating, e.g., fixing a leak that threatens the structure): reimbursable **even without the landlord's authorization**, with a **right of retention** (staying in the property until reimbursed) (Law 8.245/1991, art. 35).
- **Useful** (they increase or ease use, e.g., a security grille, a shower enclosure): reimbursable **only if authorized**, also with retention (art. 35).
- **Luxury** (voluptuary: decoration or ornament, e.g., decorative wallpaper): **not reimbursed**; the tenant can remove them when leaving, if that does not damage the structure (art. 36).
- All of this applies **unless an express clause says otherwise** (art. 35). A clause **waiving reimbursement and retention is valid** (STJ Precedent 335). Classify it as **attention**: it is legal, but the tenant must know they will not be reimbursed.
- For the tenant: ask for authorization **in writing** before any work (art. 23, VI requires prior written consent to modify the property) and keep the invoices.

## Right of first refusal when the rented property is sold (arts. 27 to 29)

- If the landlord is going to **sell**, promise to sell, assign or give the property in payment, the tenant has a **right of first refusal** to buy **on the same terms** offered to third parties (art. 27).
- The landlord must communicate **all the terms**: price, payment method, encumbrances on the property, and the place and time to see the documents (art. 27, sole paragraph).
- The tenant has **30 days** to accept in full, unequivocally; otherwise they lose the right of first refusal (art. 28).
- If the landlord backs out after acceptance, they are liable for the losses (art. 29).
- There is no right of first refusal in a court-ordered sale, a swap, a donation or a capital contribution, among others (art. 32).
- The tenant must allow visits by interested buyers, at an agreed day and time (art. 23, IX).

## Tenant passed over on first refusal: what they can do (art. 33)

- They can claim **losses and damages** from the seller (art. 33).
- They can **take the property** by depositing the price and expenses, within **6 months** of the registration of the sale, **provided the contract was registered in the property's title record (matrícula) at least 30 days before the sale** (art. 33). Registration (averbação) requires a contract signed also by **two witnesses** (art. 33, sole paragraph).
- Advice for a tenant who wants this protection: a contract with two witnesses and registration in the property's title record (notary fees apply).

## Clause waiving the right of first refusal

Clauses in which the tenant "waives the right of first refusal" appear in standard-form contracts. Since the right of first refusal is a right granted by law and art. 45 voids clauses that defeat the purposes of the law, treat it as **high attention**: its validity is debated, and in a standard-form contract an advance waiver of a right that results from the nature of the deal is void (Civil Code, art. 424). Recommend removing the clause or seeking professional advice if buying the property interests the tenant.
