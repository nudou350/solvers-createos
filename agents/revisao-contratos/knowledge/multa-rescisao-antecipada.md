# Penalty for early termination of a lease (art. 4 of the Tenancy Law)

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Penalty for leaving before the end of the contract: the rule of art. 4

- During the agreed term, **the landlord cannot ask for the property back** (Law 8.245/1991, art. 4, first sentence). The exceptions are in art. 9 (agreement, breach, non-payment, urgent repairs required by public authorities).
- **The tenant can leave early**, paying the agreed penalty, which must be **proportional to the period of the contract already served** (art. 4, as worded by Law 12.744/2012).
- If the contract does not set a penalty, the one the judge sets applies (art. 4, final part).
- The law **does not set the amount of the full penalty**. The market custom is 3 months' rent, but that is practice, not a legal rule. A manifestly excessive penalty can be reduced by the judge (Civil Code, art. 413).
- A clause that demands the **full** penalty, with no proportionality, contradicts art. 4 and is void in that part (art. 45). In practice, the tenant pays only the proportional part.

## Formula for the proportional early termination penalty

```
proportional penalty = full penalty × (months remaining ÷ total contract term in months)
months remaining     = total term − months already served
```

- The calculation can also be done in **days** when the contract provides for it (more precise when the exit falls in the middle of a month).
- Always use the **current rent** (already adjusted) if the contract says "penalty of X months' current rent".
- Charges (condominium, IPTU) normally **do not** go into the penalty base, unless the contract says "rent and charges". Read the clause.

## Numerical examples of the proportional penalty (art. 4)

**Example 1: 30-month contract.** Rent of R$ 2,000, penalty of 3 months' rent (R$ 6,000). The tenant served 12 months and leaves.
- 30 − 12 = 18 months remain.
- Penalty = 6,000 × 18 ÷ 30 = **R$ 3,600**.

**Example 2: leaving near the end.** Same contract, leaving after 27 months served.
- 3 months remain. Penalty = 6,000 × 3 ÷ 30 = **R$ 600**.

**Example 3: 12-month contract.** Rent of R$ 1,500, penalty of 3 months' rent (R$ 4,500). Leaving after 4 months.
- 8 months remain. Penalty = 4,500 × 8 ÷ 12 = **R$ 3,000**.

**Example 4: 36-month contract, rent of R$ 3,200, penalty of 3 months' rent (R$ 9,600), leaving after 20 months.**
- 16 months remain. Penalty = 9,600 × 16 ÷ 36 = **R$ 4,266.67**.

Always check the calculation against the numbers in the user's contract and show the step by step.

## Penalty waiver for a job transfer (art. 4, sole paragraph)

The tenant **does not pay a penalty** when (Law 8.245/1991, art. 4, sole paragraph):
1. the exit results from a **transfer by the employer** (private or public) to work in **another location**, different from where the contract began; **and**
2. they **notify the landlord in writing at least 30 days in advance**.

Practical tips:
- Keep proof of the transfer (a letter or e-mail from the employer, an official order, a new employment contract).
- Give the notice in writing with proof of receipt (e-mail with a reply, letter with return receipt, notice through a notary's office).
- Without the 30-day notice, the waiver can be challenged.
- The rule speaks of an employer: a self-employed person or someone who moves on their own is **not** covered by the sole paragraph and pays the proportional penalty.

## When there is no penalty at all: contract already extended for an indefinite term

If the contract's term **has already ended** and the tenant stayed in the property, the lease becomes **indefinite-term** (art. 46, par. 1, for contracts of 30 months or more; art. 47, for shorter contracts). In that case:
- there is no longer a "term to serve", so **the penalty of art. 4 does not apply**;
- the tenant can leave with **30 days' written notice** (art. 6);
- without the notice, the landlord can charge **one month of rent and charges** (art. 6, sole paragraph).

Details in "30-month contract, extension and termination without cause".

## Termination penalty paid by the landlord

The landlord **cannot ask for the property before the end of the term** (art. 4), except in the cases of art. 9. A clause that gives the landlord the right to take the property back "at any time, by paying a penalty" during the fixed term contradicts art. 4 and should be treated as **high attention / possibly void** (art. 45). If the landlord themselves wants to leave the contract, it can only be by agreement (art. 9, I).
