# Eviction for non-payment: injunction, curing the default and deadlines

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Eviction only through a court action (art. 5)

- Whatever the reason, the landlord can only recover the property through an **eviction action** (ação de despejo) (Law 8.245/1991, art. 5). Changing the lock, cutting off water or electricity, or removing the tenant's furniture on their own is **not allowed**.
- Non-payment of rent and charges is grounds for ending the lease (art. 9, III).

## Eviction injunction in 15 days (art. 59, par. 1)

- The judge grants an **injunction to vacate in 15 days**, without hearing the tenant, with a deposit of 3 months' rent posted by the landlord, in specific cases (art. 59, par. 1), among them:
  - breach of a written move-out agreement signed with two witnesses and a minimum term of 6 months (item I);
  - end of a short-stay rental, with the action filed within 30 days (item III);
  - failure to provide a new guarantee within the deadline of art. 40 (item VII);
  - **non-payment in a contract with no guarantee at all** (item IX).
- In the case of item IX, the tenant **avoids termination and the injunction** if, within the 15 days, they **deposit in court everything they owe** (art. 59, par. 3).

## Curing the default (purgação da mora): paying to avoid eviction (art. 62)

- In an eviction action for non-payment, the tenant and the guarantor can **avoid termination** by paying, within **15 days from the summons**, the updated debt through a **court deposit**, including (art. 62, II):
  - rent and charges due up to the deposit;
  - enforceable contractual fines;
  - default interest;
  - court costs and the landlord's lawyer's **10% fees**, if the contract does not set another amount.
- If the landlord says the deposit was insufficient, the tenant can **top it up within 10 days** (art. 62, III).
- Rent that falls due during the proceedings must be deposited in court (art. 62, V).
- **Limit**: the tenant cannot cure the default if they already used this option in the **24 months** before the action (art. 62, sole paragraph).

## Deadlines after the eviction ruling (arts. 63 to 65)

- Once eviction is ordered, the deadline for voluntary move-out is **30 days**; it drops to **15 days** if more than 4 months passed between the summons and the ruling, or if the eviction was for non-payment or breach (art. 9) or for termination without cause (art. 46, par. 2) (art. 63, par. 1).
- When the deadline ends, the eviction is carried out, with force if needed (art. 65).
- The eviction **cannot be carried out until the 30th day after the death** of the spouse, ascendant, descendant or sibling of the person living in the property (art. 65, par. 2); breaking this rule is a crime (art. 44, IV).

## What to tell someone who received an eviction summons

1. **Seek a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública) immediately**: the 15-day deadline runs from the summons.
2. Add up the debt with fine, interest and 10% fees (art. 62, II) and see whether the deposit is possible.
3. Check whether the default was already cured in the last 24 months (art. 62, sole paragraph).
4. Keep depositing the rent that falls due (art. 62, V).
5. Keep all proof of payment.
