# The CDC in leases: when the Consumer Defense Code applies

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## The CDC does not apply to the lease between tenant and landlord

- The case law of the **STJ (Brazil's superior court for federal law) is settled** that the **Consumer Defense Code (CDC) does not apply to leases** governed by Law 8.245/1991: a lease has its own law and lacks the traits of a consumer relationship. Examples often cited: REsp 239.578/SP (2000), REsp 605.295/MG (2009) and AgRg no AREsp 41.062/GO (2013).
- Practical consequences:
  - the CDC's **2% late-payment fine cap** (art. 52, par. 1) **does not apply** to rent;
  - protection comes from the Tenancy Law itself (void clauses, art. 45; prohibited charges, art. 43) and from the Civil Code, applied where the law is silent (Law 8.245/1991, art. 79).
- Even without the CDC, in a standard-form contract (the tenant only signs the ready-made template), ambiguous clauses are interpreted in favor of the party who adhered (Civil Code, art. 423), and an advance waiver of a right that results from the nature of the deal is void (art. 424). Excessive penalties can be reduced (art. 413).

## The CDC and the real estate agency: where it may apply

- The **management** contract between the **landlord and the agency** is a service agreement, and the STJ has applied the CDC to that relationship, treating the landlord as a consumer of the manager's service (REsp 509.304/PR, 3rd Panel, 2013).
- Toward the **tenant**, the agency acts as the landlord's **intermediary**. Some decisions apply the CDC to the **services the agency provides directly** to the tenant (customer service, charging of fees, advertising of the property), but the **lease itself** remains outside the CDC. The topic is not entirely settled: write with caution and recommend a lawyer or Procon (the consumer protection agency) in a concrete case.
- Regardless of the CDC, the Tenancy Law already places brokerage and credit-check fees on the **landlord** (art. 22, VII), and charging the tenant an amount beyond the rent and charges is a misdemeanor (art. 43, I).

## How to talk about this with the user

- Do not say "the CDC protects you in a lease". Say: "In a lease between private individuals, the STJ holds that the CDC does not apply; protection comes from the Tenancy Law and the Civil Code."
- If the complaint is against the **agency** (undue fee, poor service, failure in management), say that the CDC may protect the user in that service relationship and that Procon and the Small Claims Court (Juizado Especial) are possible routes.
