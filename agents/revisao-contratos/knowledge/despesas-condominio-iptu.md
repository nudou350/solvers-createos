# Expenses: ordinary vs extraordinary condominium costs, IPTU and utility bills (arts. 22 to 26)

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Ordinary condominium expenses: paid by the tenant (art. 23, XII and par. 1)

The tenant pays the **ordinary expenses**, tied to day-to-day administration (Law 8.245/1991, art. 23, XII and par. 1), especially:
- salaries and charges of the condominium staff;
- water, electricity, gas and power for the common areas;
- cleaning, upkeep and **painting of internal common areas**;
- maintenance of equipment, elevators, intercom, shared antenna and leisure areas;
- small repairs to the common installations;
- sharing of a deficit and **replenishment of the reserve fund used for ordinary expenses**, unless it relates to a period before the lease.

Condition: the tenant is only obliged to pay if there is a **budget forecast and monthly apportionment that can be proven**, and they can ask for proof at any time (art. 23, par. 2; art. 22, IX).

## Extraordinary condominium expenses: paid by the landlord (art. 22, X)

The landlord pays the **extraordinary expenses**, which are not routine costs (art. 22, X and sole paragraph), especially:
- a) renovation works or additions to the building's structure;
- b) **painting of facades**, side walls, ventilation shafts and external frames;
- c) works to restore habitability conditions;
- d) labor indemnities for dismissals that happened before the lease;
- e) **installation** of security, fire, telephone, intercom, sports and leisure equipment;
- f) decoration and landscaping of the common areas;
- g) **creation of the reserve fund** (forming the fund; replenishment after ordinary use is the tenant's).

A clause that shifts the extraordinary expenses to the tenant contradicts art. 22, X, and must be classified as **illegal/abusive** (art. 45). Unlike IPTU, item X does not have the caveat "unless otherwise provided".

## IPTU, fees and fire insurance (art. 22, VIII)

- By law, **property taxes and fees (IPTU, garbage fee) and the supplementary fire insurance** are the landlord's, **unless the contract expressly says otherwise** (art. 22, VIII).
- It is **allowed** and common for the contract to transfer the IPTU to the tenant, as long as it does so **expressly**. Classify it as **ok** if it is clear, or **attention** if it is vague (e.g., "charges in general").
- The landlord can charge IPTU and condominium together with the month's rent (art. 25). If they pay in full up front at a discount, the discount is theirs, unless the tenant reimburses the full amount (art. 25, sole paragraph).
- Tip: confirm whether the IPTU comes in installments or in a single payment and whether there are old debts (debts from before the lease are not the tenant's).

## Utility bills and documents (art. 23, VII and VIII)

- Water, electricity, gas, phone and internet used by the unit: **tenant** (art. 23, VIII).
- The tenant must hand the landlord any tax and condominium bills and any notices they receive (art. 23, VII).
- The landlord must show proof of what they charge (art. 22, IX) and give an itemized receipt (art. 22, VI).

## Urgent repairs and rent reduction (art. 26)

- Urgent repairs that are the landlord's responsibility must be allowed by the tenant (art. 26).
- If they last **more than 10 days**, the tenant is entitled to a **proportional rent reduction** for the excess period; if they last **more than 30 days**, the tenant can end the contract (art. 26, sole paragraph).
- Defects that predate the lease are the landlord's responsibility (art. 22, IV). Damage caused by the tenant, family members or visitors is the tenant's (art. 23, V).
