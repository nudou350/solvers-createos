# Advance rent and charges a tenant cannot be asked to pay (arts. 20, 22 and 43)

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Advance rent is prohibited, with exceptions (art. 20)

- Rule: the landlord **cannot demand rent in advance** (Law 8.245/1991, art. 20).
- Rent, as a rule, falls due **after** the month of use: if the contract does not set another date, by the **6th business day of the month after the one that has ended** (art. 23, I). The contract can set another due day, but it cannot demand the month that is still to be used if there is a guarantee.
- **Exceptions** where paying in advance is allowed:
  1. **a contract with no guarantee at all**: the landlord can charge up to the 6th business day of the month that is starting (art. 42);
  2. **short-stay rental** (up to 90 days): the landlord can receive everything at once and in advance (arts. 48 and 49).
- Prohibited example: a contract with a guarantor or rental guarantee insurance that demands the "first rent on signing" for the month that is about to begin.

## Charging advance rent is a misdemeanor (art. 43, III)

It is a **criminal misdemeanor** (art. 43), punishable by simple imprisonment of 5 days to 6 months **or a fine of 3 to 12 times the last rent, paid to the tenant**:
- I: demanding, because of the lease, **an amount beyond the rent and the permitted charges**;
- II: demanding **more than one guarantee**;
- III: **charging advance rent**, except under art. 42 and for short-stay rentals.

In practice, the first step is to ask in writing for the clause to be corrected. If the tenant has already paid, they can ask for the amount back or for it to be offset against the following rents. For high amounts or a refusal: a lawyer, the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública) or the Small Claims Civil Court (Juizado Especial Cível).

## Fees that belong to the landlord and cannot be passed on (art. 22, VII)

- The landlord must pay the **real estate agency's management fees** and **brokerage (intermediation) fees**, including the costs of **checking the reliability** of the tenant or guarantor (credit and background check) (art. 22, VII).
- For that reason, a "contract fee", "registration fee" or "brokerage fee" charged to the tenant is, as a rule, an **undue charge** (art. 22, VII, combined with art. 43, I).
- Costs the tenant may pay if the contract provides for them: the rental guarantee insurance premium (art. 23, XI), water, electricity and gas consumption (art. 23, VIII), ordinary condominium expenses (art. 23, XII) and IPTU (municipal property tax) by express clause (art. 22, VIII).
- Receipt: the landlord must give an **itemized receipt**, with no blanket release of debt (art. 22, VI).

## Key money ("luvas") and off-the-books amounts in residential leases

Any amount charged "to close the deal" (key money, "reservation", "key fee") beyond the rent and the permitted charges counts as an amount beyond what is allowed (art. 43, I). It is a warning sign for the tenant. Recommend asking for a receipt for everything and not paying in cash without proof.
