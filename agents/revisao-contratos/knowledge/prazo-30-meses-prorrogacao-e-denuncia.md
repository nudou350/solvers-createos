# 30-month contract, extension for an indefinite term and termination without cause (denúncia vazia)

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Why 30 months matters in a residential lease

Law 8.245/1991 treats the residential contract differently depending on the term:
- **30 months or more, in writing** (art. 46): at the end of the term, the contract ends with no notice needed; if it becomes indefinite-term, the landlord can ask for the property back with 30 days' notice, **without needing a reason** ("denúncia vazia", art. 46, par. 2).
- **Less than 30 months or a verbal contract** (art. 47): at the end of the term, the contract is automatically extended for an indefinite term, and the landlord **can only take the property back in the cases the law provides** (art. 47, I to V), such as own use or when the lease passes **5 uninterrupted years** (art. 47, V).

That is why landlords often prefer 30 months: it is what allows them to take the property back without justifying it. For the tenant, a shorter contract gives more stability after expiry.

## Landlord's termination without cause (art. 46, par. 2)

- It applies to a **written** contract of **30 months or more**, already expired and extended for an indefinite term (the tenant stayed more than 30 days without objection, art. 46, par. 1).
- The landlord can "terminate" (end) at any time, giving **30 days to vacate** (art. 46, par. 2).
- If the tenant does not leave, the route is an **eviction action** (art. 5). If the tenant agrees to leave in their defense, the judge gives **6 months** to vacate and the tenant is exempt from court costs and fees if they leave within the deadline (art. 61).
- On other terms, the extension keeps the contract's clauses (art. 46, par. 1), including the guarantee (art. 39), unless a clause says otherwise.

## Taking the property back in a contract shorter than 30 months (art. 47)

After expiry, once the contract is extended, the landlord can only take the property back (art. 47):
- I: in the cases of art. 9 (agreement, breach, non-payment, urgent repairs);
- II: end of the employment contract, if the housing was tied to the job;
- III: **own use**, or use by a spouse or partner, or by an ascendant/descendant who has no residential property of their own;
- IV: demolition or approved works that increase the area by at least 20%;
- V: when the lease exceeds **5 uninterrupted years**.

Someone who asks for own use and does not use the property within 180 days, or does not use it for at least 1 year, commits a crime and may pay a fine of 12 to 24 months' rent to the former tenant (art. 44, II and sole paragraph).

A clause that prohibits the extension of art. 47 is **void** (art. 45).

## Tenant leaves with 30 days' notice, no penalty (art. 6)

- In a contract for an **indefinite term** (including an extended one), the tenant can leave at any time, giving **30 days' written notice** (art. 6).
- **There is no penalty under art. 4**, because there is no longer a term to serve.
- Without the notice, the landlord can demand **one month of rent and charges** at the current amounts (art. 6, sole paragraph).
- Simple notice template: "I inform you that I will vacate the property at [address] on [date, at least 30 days ahead], under art. 6 of Law 8.245/1991. Please schedule the move-out inspection."

## Sale of the property during the lease (art. 8)

- If the property is sold, the buyer can end the lease by giving **90 days** to vacate (art. 8), with the request made within 90 days of the registration of the sale (art. 8, par. 2).
- Exception: a **fixed-term** contract with a **validity clause in case of sale** that is **registered in the property's title record (matrícula)** (art. 8). For the tenant, asking for this clause and registering the contract is real protection.
- See also the right of first refusal (arts. 27 to 34).

## Term of 10 years or more and the spouse's signature (art. 3)

A contract of **10 years or more** depends on the **spouse's consent** (authorization from the landlord's spouse, "vênia conjugal"). Without it, the spouse is not bound to respect the part of the term that exceeds 10 years (art. 3, sole paragraph).
