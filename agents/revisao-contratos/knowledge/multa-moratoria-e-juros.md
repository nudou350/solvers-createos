# Late rent: late fine, interest and inflation adjustment (Law 14.905/2024)

> Updated October 2026. General guidance on urban residential leases in Brazil (Law 8.245/1991), not legal advice: it does not replace the advice of a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública).

## Late-payment fine on rent: how much is reasonable

- The Tenancy Law **does not set** a percentage for the late fine. The fine is a penalty clause under the Civil Code (arts. 408 and 411), and the contract can charge it together with the rent owed (art. 411).
- The **2% limit of the Consumer Defense Code** (CDC, art. 52, par. 1) **does not apply** to a lease between private individuals, because the STJ holds that a lease is governed by Law 8.245 and is not a consumer relationship (see "The CDC in leases").
- In practice, a **10%** fine on the overdue amount is the most common and is usually accepted by the courts. Higher percentages (15%, 20%) are **attention**: the judge must reduce a **manifestly excessive** penalty (Civil Code, art. 413), and the fine can never exceed the value of the main obligation (art. 412).
- The late fine (default fine) is different from the early termination penalty (art. 4 of Law 8.245). One does not replace the other.
- A punctuality discount ("abono") that in practice hides a high fine is a point of **attention**: add the lost discount to the fine to see the real cost of paying late.

## Default interest after Law 14.905/2024 (Civil Code, art. 406)

- Law 14.905/2024 changed the Civil Code (effects in force from August 30, 2024):
  - **Inflation adjustment**: if the contract does not define an index, the **IPCA** is used (Civil Code, art. 389, sole paragraph).
  - **Legal interest**: when the contract does not set a rate, the **legal rate = Selic minus IPCA** applies (art. 406, par. 1), calculated by the National Monetary Council (CMN) and published by the Central Bank (art. 406, par. 2). If the result is negative, the interest for the period is zero (art. 406, par. 3).
- **The contract can agree on default interest.** The custom for rent is **1% per month**. Between individuals, the Usury Law (Decree 22.626/1933) still applies, which prohibits interest above **double the legal rate** (art. 1); Law 14.905 lifted this limit only for certain obligations, such as those contracted **between legal entities** or with financial institutions (Law 14.905/2024, art. 3).
- After Law 14.905 there is debate about how to calculate this ceiling with the new legal rate. Classify interest above **1% per month** as **attention** and recommend confirming with a lawyer; 1% per month interest is usually accepted.
- The methodology is in CMN Resolution No. 5.171/2024: the Central Bank publishes the legal rate every month (the month's Selic minus the previous month's IPCA-15), applied as simple interest. The figure changes each period: check the Central Bank's Citizen's Calculator and do not estimate it (verify at the official source).

## Example of a late rent calculation

Rent of R$ 2,000 paid 20 days late; contract with a 10% fine, 1% per month interest and IPCA adjustment:
- Fine: 2,000 × 10% = **R$ 200**.
- Interest: 1% per month for 20 days ≈ 2,000 × 1% × 20/30 = **R$ 13.33** (simple interest, pro rata).
- Adjustment: by the IPCA for the period (small for short delays).
- Approximate total: **R$ 2,213.33** plus the adjustment.

## Lawyer's fees charged on late payment

- A clause that charges **lawyer's fees** for late payment **without a lawsuit** (friendly collection) is a point of **attention**: there must be legal work actually performed and a reasonable amount.
- In an eviction action for non-payment, to avoid termination the tenant deposits the debt with fine, interest, court costs and **10% fees**, if the contract does not set another percentage (Law 8.245/1991, art. 62, II, "d").
