# Law 8.245/1991: lease guarantees (arts. 37 to 42)

> Updated September 2026. This does not replace the advice of a lawyer.

Official text of Law No. 8.245 of October 18, 1991 (Brazilian Tenancy Law, "Lei do Inquilinato"), according to the compilation of the Presidency of the Republic (planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8245.htm), consulted on 2026-09-30. Repealed passages were omitted; the notes "(Wording given by...)" and "(Added by...)" come from the compilation itself. Latest amendments to this law up to the date consulted: Law 12.112/2009 and Law 12.744/2012. The wording below is an unofficial English translation of the official Portuguese text; the Portuguese original is the only authoritative version. Legal texts are not protected by copyright (Law 9.610/1998, art. 8, IV).

## Art. 37 of the Tenancy Law: types of guarantee and prohibition of more than one guarantee

Art. 37. In the lease contract, the landlord can require the following types of guarantee from the tenant:

I - security deposit (caução);

II - surety (fiança);

III - rental guarantee insurance (seguro de fiança locatícia).

IV - fiduciary assignment of investment fund shares. (Added by Law No. 11.196 of 2005)

Sole paragraph. More than one of the types of guarantee in the same lease contract is prohibited, on pain of nullity.

## Art. 38 of the Tenancy Law: security deposit and the three-month rent limit

Art. 38. The security deposit can be in movable or immovable property.

Par. 1. A security deposit in movable property must be registered at the registry of deeds and documents; one in immovable property must be annotated in the margin of the respective title record (matrícula).

Par. 2. A cash security deposit, which cannot exceed the equivalent of three months' rent, will be placed in a savings account authorized by the Public Authority and regulated by it, with all the advantages arising from it going to the tenant when the respective sum is withdrawn.

Par. 3. A security deposit in securities and shares must be replaced, within thirty days, in case of composition with creditors, bankruptcy or liquidation of the issuing companies.

## Art. 39 of the Tenancy Law: the guarantee lasts until the property is returned

Art. 39. Unless the contract provides otherwise, any of the lease guarantees extends until the actual return of the property, even if the lease is extended for an indefinite term by force of this Law. (Wording given by Law No. 12.112 of 2009)

## Art. 40 of the Tenancy Law: replacing the guarantor or the guarantee, and release of the guarantor

Art. 40. The landlord can demand a new guarantor or the replacement of the type of guarantee, in the following cases:

I - death of the guarantor;

II – absence, interdiction, judicial reorganization, bankruptcy or insolvency of the guarantor, judicially declared; (Wording given by Law No. 12.112 of 2009)

III - sale or encumbrance of all the guarantor's real estate or their change of residence without notice to the landlord;

IV - release of the guarantor;

V - extension of the lease for an indefinite term, when the surety was agreed for a fixed term;

VI - disappearance of the movable assets;

VII - expropriation or sale of the property.

VIII - release of a guarantee constituted by investment fund shares; (Added by Law No. 11.196 of 2005)

IX - liquidation or closing of the investment fund referred to in item IV of art. 37 of this Law. (Added by Law No. 11.196 of 2005)

X – extension of the lease for an indefinite term once the landlord has been notified by the guarantor of their intention to be released, the guarantor remaining bound by all the effects of the surety for 120 (one hundred and twenty) days after notification to the landlord. (Added by Law No. 12.112 of 2009)

Sole paragraph. The landlord can notify the tenant to provide a new lease guarantee within 30 (thirty) days, on pain of termination of the lease. (Added by Law No. 12.112 of 2009)

#### Art. 41 of the Tenancy Law: rental guarantee insurance

Art. 41. Rental guarantee insurance will cover all of the tenant's obligations.

## Art. 42 of the Tenancy Law: lease with no guarantee: rent up to the sixth business day of the upcoming month

Art. 42. When the lease is not guaranteed by any of the types, the landlord can demand from the tenant payment of rent and charges up to the sixth business day of the upcoming month.
