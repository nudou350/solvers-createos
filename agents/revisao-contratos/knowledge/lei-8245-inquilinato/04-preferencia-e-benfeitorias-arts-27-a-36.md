# Law 8.245/1991: right of first refusal and improvements (arts. 27 to 36)

> Updated September 2026. This does not replace the advice of a lawyer.

Official text of Law No. 8.245 of October 18, 1991 (Brazilian Tenancy Law, "Lei do Inquilinato"), according to the compilation of the Presidency of the Republic (planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8245.htm), consulted on 2026-09-30. Repealed passages were omitted; the notes "(Wording given by...)" and "(Added by...)" come from the compilation itself. Latest amendments to this law up to the date consulted: Law 12.112/2009 and Law 12.744/2012. The wording below is an unofficial English translation of the official Portuguese text; the Portuguese original is the only authoritative version. Legal texts are not protected by copyright (Law 9.610/1998, art. 8, IV).

## Art. 27 of the Tenancy Law: the tenant's right of first refusal on a sale

Art. 27. In case of sale, promise of sale, assignment or promise of assignment of rights, or giving in payment, the tenant has a right of first refusal to acquire the leased property, on equal terms with third parties, and the landlord must make the deal known to them by judicial or extrajudicial notification or another means of unequivocal notice.

Sole paragraph. The communication must contain all the terms of the deal and, in particular, the price, the form of payment, the existence of encumbrances (real liens), as well as the place and time at which the relevant documentation can be examined.

## Art. 28 of the Tenancy Law: 30 days to exercise the right of first refusal

Art. 28. The tenant's right of first refusal lapses if their full acceptance of the proposal is not unequivocally expressed within thirty days.

## Art. 29 of the Tenancy Law: landlord backing out after acceptance

Art. 29. Once the tenant has accepted the proposal, the landlord's later withdrawal from the deal makes the landlord liable for the losses caused, including lost profits.

## Art. 30 of the Tenancy Law: the subtenant's right of first refusal

Art. 30. When the property is sublet in its entirety, the right of first refusal belongs to the subtenant and, after them, to the tenant. If there are several subtenants, the right of first refusal belongs to all of them jointly, or to any of them, if only one is interested.

Sole paragraph. If there are several applicants, the right of first refusal belongs to the oldest tenant and, if of the same date, to the eldest.

## Art. 31 of the Tenancy Law: sale of several units

Art. 31. In the case of the sale of more than one real estate unit, the right of first refusal applies to all of the assets being sold.

## Art. 32 of the Tenancy Law: cases with no right of first refusal

Art. 32. The right of first refusal does not extend to cases of loss of ownership or sale by court decision, swap, donation, capital contribution, spin-off, merger and incorporation.

Sole paragraph. In contracts signed from October 1, 2001, the right of first refusal referred to in this article also does not extend to cases of creation of fiduciary ownership and of loss of ownership or sale by any form of enforcement of a guarantee, including through extrajudicial auction, and this condition must expressly appear in a specific contractual clause, set apart from the others by its graphic presentation. (Added by Law No. 10.931 of 2004)

## Art. 33 of the Tenancy Law: tenant passed over on first refusal: damages or taking the property

Art. 33. A tenant passed over in their right of first refusal can claim losses and damages from the seller or, by depositing the price and other expenses of the transfer, take the leased property for themselves, if they request it within six months, counted from the registration of the act at the real estate registry, provided that the lease contract was registered in the property's title record (matrícula) at least thirty days before the sale.

Sole paragraph. Registration will be made upon presentation of any copy of the lease contract as long as it is also signed by two witnesses.

#### Art. 34 of the Tenancy Law: right of first refusal of a co-owner

Art. 34. When the property is co-owned, the co-owner's right of first refusal takes priority over the tenant's.

## Art. 35 of the Tenancy Law: necessary and useful improvements: compensation and retention

Art. 35. Unless the contract expressly provides otherwise, the necessary improvements made by the tenant, even if not authorized by the landlord, as well as the useful ones, provided they are authorized, will be compensable and allow the exercise of the right of retention.

## Art. 36 of the Tenancy Law: luxury (voluptuary) improvements

Art. 36. Luxury (voluptuary) improvements will not be compensable, and can be removed by the tenant when the lease ends, as long as their removal does not affect the structure and substance of the property.
