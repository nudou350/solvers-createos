# Law 8.245/1991: residential and short-stay leases (arts. 46 to 50)

> Updated September 2026. This does not replace the advice of a lawyer.

Official text of Law No. 8.245 of October 18, 1991 (Brazilian Tenancy Law, "Lei do Inquilinato"), according to the compilation of the Presidency of the Republic (planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8245.htm), consulted on 2026-09-30. Repealed passages were omitted; the notes "(Wording given by...)" and "(Added by...)" come from the compilation itself. Latest amendments to this law up to the date consulted: Law 12.112/2009 and Law 12.744/2012. The wording below is an unofficial English translation of the official Portuguese text; the Portuguese original is the only authoritative version. Legal texts are not protected by copyright (Law 9.610/1998, art. 8, IV).

## Art. 46 of the Tenancy Law: contract of 30 months or more: end of term and termination without cause

Art. 46. In leases agreed in writing and for a term of thirty months or more, the contract ends when the stipulated term ends, without any notification or notice being required.

Par. 1. Once the agreed term has ended, if the tenant remains in possession of the rented property for more than thirty days without opposition from the landlord, the lease is presumed extended for an indefinite term, with the other clauses and conditions of the contract maintained.

Par. 2. Once the extension has occurred, the landlord can terminate the contract at any time, granting thirty days to vacate.

## Art. 47 of the Tenancy Law: contract shorter than 30 months: extension and cases of taking the property back

Art. 47. When agreed verbally or in writing and for a term shorter than thirty months, once the established term has ended, the lease is automatically extended, for an indefinite term, and the property can only be taken back:

I - in the cases of art. 9;

II - as a result of the end of the employment contract, if the tenant's occupation of the property is related to their job;

III - if requested for own use, or for use by their spouse or partner, or for residential use by an ascendant or descendant who, like their spouse or partner, does not have a residential property of their own;

IV - if requested for demolition and licensed construction or for works approved by the Public Authority that increase the built area by at least twenty percent or, if the property is used to operate a hotel or guesthouse, by fifty percent;

V - if the uninterrupted duration of the lease exceeds five years.

Par. 1. In the case of item III, the need must be demonstrated in court, if:

a) the party taking the property back, claiming a need to use it, is occupying, for the same purpose, another property they own in the same locality or, living in or using someone else's property, has already taken the property back before;

b) the ascendant or descendant who benefits from the retaking lives in a property of their own.

Par. 2. In the cases of items III and IV, the party taking the property back must prove that they are the owner, promissory buyer or promissory assignee, irrevocably, with possession of the property and a title registered in its title record (matrícula).

## Art. 48 of the Tenancy Law: short-stay rental (up to 90 days)

Art. 48. A short-stay rental is one intended for the temporary residence of the tenant, for leisure, taking courses, health treatment, carrying out works on their own property, and other circumstances that stem only from a given period of time, and contracted for a term not exceeding ninety days, whether or not the property is furnished.

Sole paragraph. If the lease involves a furnished property, the contract must include the description of the furniture and utensils that furnish it, as well as the condition they are in.

## Art. 49 of the Tenancy Law: short-stay: advance rent allowed

Art. 49. The landlord can receive the rent and charges all at once and in advance, as well as demand any of the types of guarantee provided for in art. 37 to cover the other obligations of the contract.

## Art. 50 of the Tenancy Law: short-stay extended for an indefinite term

Art. 50. Once the agreed term has ended, if the tenant remains in the property without opposition from the landlord for more than thirty days, the lease is presumed extended for an indefinite time, and advance payment of rent and charges can no longer be demanded.

Sole paragraph. Once the extension has occurred, the landlord can only terminate the contract after thirty months from its start or in the cases of art. 47.
