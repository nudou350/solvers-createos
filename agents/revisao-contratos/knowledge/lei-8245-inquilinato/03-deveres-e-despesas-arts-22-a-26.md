# Law 8.245/1991: duties of the landlord and the tenant, condominium expenses (arts. 22 to 26)

> Updated September 2026. This does not replace the advice of a lawyer.

Official text of Law No. 8.245 of October 18, 1991 (Brazilian Tenancy Law, "Lei do Inquilinato"), according to the compilation of the Presidency of the Republic (planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8245.htm), consulted on 2026-09-30. Repealed passages were omitted; the notes "(Wording given by...)" and "(Added by...)" come from the compilation itself. Latest amendments to this law up to the date consulted: Law 12.112/2009 and Law 12.744/2012. The wording below is an unofficial English translation of the official Portuguese text; the Portuguese original is the only authoritative version. Legal texts are not protected by copyright (Law 9.610/1998, art. 8, IV).

## Art. 22 of the Tenancy Law: duties of the landlord, extraordinary condominium expenses and IPTU

Art. 22. The landlord is obliged to:

I - deliver the rented property to the tenant in a condition fit for the use for which it is intended;

II - guarantee, throughout the lease, peaceful use of the leased property;

III - maintain, throughout the lease, the form and purpose of the property;

IV - be liable for flaws or defects that predate the lease;

V - provide the tenant, if they request it, with a detailed description of the condition of the property at the time of its delivery, with express reference to any existing defects;

VI - provide the tenant with an itemized receipt for the amounts they pay, a blanket release being prohibited;

VII - pay the real estate management fees, if any, and intermediation fees, which include the expenses necessary to assess the reliability of the applicant or their guarantor;

VIII - pay the taxes and fees, and also the premium of the supplementary fire insurance, that fall or come to fall on the property, unless the contract expressly provides otherwise;

IX - show the tenant, when requested, the proof of the amounts being demanded;

X - pay the extraordinary condominium expenses.

### Art. 22, sole paragraph of the Tenancy Law: what extraordinary condominium expenses are

Art. 22, sole paragraph. Extraordinary condominium expenses are understood to be those that do not relate to the routine costs of maintaining the building, especially:

a) renovation works or additions that concern the overall structure of the property;

b) painting of facades, side walls, ventilation and lighting shafts, as well as external frames;

c) works intended to restore the habitability conditions of the building;

d) labor and social security indemnities for the dismissal of employees, occurring on a date before the start of the lease;

e) installation of security and fire equipment, telephony, intercom, sports and leisure equipment;

f) decoration and landscaping expenses in the common-use areas;

g) creation of a reserve fund.

## Art. 23 of the Tenancy Law: duties of the tenant and ordinary condominium expenses

Art. 23. The tenant is obliged to:

I - pay on time the rent and the charges of the lease, legally or contractually due, within the stipulated period or, failing that, by the sixth business day of the month following the one that has ended, at the leased property, when another place has not been indicated in the contract;

II - use the property for the agreed or presumed use, compatible with its nature and with the purpose for which it is intended, treating it with the same care as if it were their own;

III - return the property, when the lease ends, in the condition in which they received it, except for deterioration resulting from its normal use;

IV - immediately bring to the landlord's attention the appearance of any damage or defect whose repair is the landlord's responsibility, as well as any disturbances by third parties;

V - immediately repair the damage found in the property, or in its installations, caused by themselves, their dependents, family members, visitors or agents;

VI - not modify the internal or external form of the property without the landlord's prior written consent;

### Art. 23, items VII to XII of the Tenancy Law: documents, inspection, utilities, rental guarantee insurance and ordinary condominium

Art. 23 (continued). VII - immediately hand over to the landlord the billing documents for taxes and condominium charges, as well as any notice, fine or demand from a public authority, even if addressed to the tenant;

VIII - pay the expenses for telephone and for consumption of power, electricity and gas, water and sewage;

IX - allow the property to be inspected by the landlord or their representative, by prior agreement on the day and time, as well as admit that it be visited and examined by third parties, in the case provided for in art. 27;

X - fully comply with the condominium convention and the internal regulations;

XI - pay the premium of the rental guarantee insurance (seguro de fiança);

XII - pay the ordinary condominium expenses.

### Art. 23, par. 1 of the Tenancy Law: what ordinary condominium expenses are

Art. 23, par. 1. Ordinary condominium expenses are understood to be those necessary for its administration, especially:

a) salaries, labor charges, social security and social contributions of the condominium's employees;

b) consumption of water and sewage, gas, electricity and power in the common-use areas;

c) cleaning, upkeep and painting of the common-use installations and areas;

d) maintenance and upkeep of the common-use hydraulic, electrical, mechanical and security installations and equipment;

e) maintenance and upkeep of the common-use installations and equipment intended for sports and leisure;

f) maintenance and upkeep of elevators, electronic doorman and shared antennas;

g) small repairs in the common-use areas and electrical and hydraulic installations;

h) apportionment of a deficit, unless it relates to a period before the start of the lease;

i) replenishment of the reserve fund, in whole or in part used to cover or supplement the expenses referred to in the previous items, unless it relates to a period before the start of the lease.

### Art. 23, pars. 2 and 3 of the Tenancy Law: the tenant only pays ordinary condominium if there is proof

Art. 23, par. 2. The tenant is obliged to pay the expenses referred to in the previous paragraph, provided the budget forecast and the monthly apportionment are proven, and they can demand proof of them at any time.

Par. 3. In a building made up of autonomous real estate units owned by the same person, the tenants are obliged to pay the expenses referred to in par. 1 of this article, provided they are proven.

## Art. 24 of the Tenancy Law: precarious collective housing

Art. 24. In properties used as multi-family collective housing, the tenants or subtenants can deposit the rent and charges in court if the construction is considered by the Public Authority to be in precarious condition.

Par. 1. Withdrawal of the deposits will only be granted upon communication, by the public authority, that the property has been regularized.

Par. 2. Tenants or subtenants who leave the property are released from the rent during the execution of the works necessary for regularization.

Par. 3. The deposits made in court by tenants and subtenants can be withdrawn, by court order, to carry out the works or services necessary to regularize the property.

## Art. 25 of the Tenancy Law: collecting taxes and condominium together with the rent

Art. 25. When responsibility for paying taxes, charges and ordinary condominium expenses is assigned to the tenant, the landlord can collect these amounts together with the rent for the month to which they refer.

Sole paragraph. If the landlord makes the payments in advance, the benefits arising from it belong to them, unless the tenant reimburses them in full.

## Art. 26 of the Tenancy Law: urgent repairs and rent reduction

Art. 26. When the property needs urgent repairs, whose performance is the landlord's responsibility, the tenant is obliged to allow them.

Sole paragraph. If the repairs last more than ten days, the tenant will be entitled to a rent reduction, proportional to the excess period; if more than thirty days, they can terminate the contract.
