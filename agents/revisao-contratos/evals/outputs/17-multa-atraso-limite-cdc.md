**About the 2% CDC cap: it probably does not apply to you.**

The case law of the STJ (Brazil's superior court for federal law) is settled that the Consumer Defense Code does not apply to leases governed by Law 8.245/1991 (for example REsp 239.578/SP and REsp 605.295/MG). So the 2% cap of the CDC (art. 52, par. 1) does not apply to rent between private individuals. The protection comes from the Tenancy Law and the Civil Code.

(If your complaint is about the real estate agency's own services, such as undue fees, the CDC may apply to that service relationship, but the lease itself stays outside it.)

**Is 10% plus 1% a month acceptable?**

- The Tenancy Law does not fix a percentage for the late fine. It is a penalty clause under the Civil Code. A **10%** fine is the most common and usually accepted by the courts. Higher percentages are attention: the judge must reduce a manifestly excessive penalty (Civil Code, art. 413) and the fine cannot exceed the main obligation (art. 412).
- Interest of **1% per month** is the custom for rent and is usually accepted. Above 1%, it is attention. If the contract set no rate, the legal rate (Selic minus IPCA, art. 406) would apply, and the Central Bank publishes it monthly.

**Things to check**

- Does the contract call for a punctuality discount ("abono") that hides a higher fine? Add the lost discount to see the real cost.
- Does it also charge lawyer's fees for late payment without a lawsuit? That is attention.
