**Short answer: the 6-month frequency is not allowed. The IGP-M itself is.**

| Item | Classification | Legal basis | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adjustment every 6 months | **ILLEGAL (void)** | Law 10.192/2001, art. 2, par. 1 and par. 3 | Ask for an annual adjustment |
| IGP-M as the index | ATTENTION (legal, but volatile) | Law 10.192/2001, art. 2; Law 8.245/1991, art. 85 | Propose the IPCA |

**What the law says**

- Any stipulation of adjustment or inflation correction with a period **shorter than one year is void by operation of law** (Law 10.192/2001, art. 2, par. 1). Devices that produce the same effect as a more frequent adjustment are also void (par. 3).
- So the adjustment can only be **annual**, counted from the start of the contract or from the last adjustment or revision (par. 2). A clause that says every 6 months is void in that part, and the rent can be adjusted only after 12 months.
- The choice of index is free as long as it is a price index. The IGP-M is legal, but it is heavily influenced by the exchange rate and wholesale prices, and is volatile (in 2021 it exceeded 30% over 12 months, and in other periods it was negative). The IPCA tends to be more stable and closer to the cost of living. It is prohibited to tie rent to a foreign currency, the exchange rate or the minimum wage (arts. 17 and 85).

**Also look for these gaps in the clause:** the base date, a replacement index if the index is discontinued and what happens with a negative change.

**Since you have not signed yet, ask for the correction. Suggested wording:**

> "The rent will be adjusted every 12 months, counted from [date], by the cumulative change in the IPCA/IBGE over the period. If the index is discontinued, the official index that replaces it will be used. In case of a negative change, the rent will remain unchanged."
