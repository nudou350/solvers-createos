**Short answer: no, not if the rent is for a month you have not used yet.**

The landlord **cannot demand rent in advance** (Law 8.245/1991, art. 20). Rent as a rule falls due **after** the month of use: if the contract sets no other date, by the 6th business day of the month after the one that ended (art. 23, I). The contract may choose another due day, but it cannot require payment for a month still to be used when there is a guarantee. Rental guarantee insurance is a guarantee (art. 37), so you are in the prohibited situation.

| Item | Classification | Legal basis | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rent due on the 1st for the month still to be used, with rental guarantee insurance | **ILLEGAL** | art. 20; art. 45 | Ask for the rent to fall due after the month of use |

**The exceptions do not apply to you**

Advance payment is allowed only in a contract with **no guarantee at all** (art. 42) and in a **short-stay rental** of up to 90 days (arts. 48 and 49). Yours has insurance, so neither fits (assuming it is a normal residential lease).

**A detail to check:** if the contract says rent is due on the 1st of the month **for the previous month** (paid after you used it), that is fine. The problem is only when the 1st-of-the-month payment covers the month you are about to live in.

**Consequences for the landlord:** charging advance rent is a criminal misdemeanor (art. 43, III), punishable by simple imprisonment of 5 days to 6 months or a fine of 3 to 12 times the last rent, paid to the tenant. If you already paid, you can ask in writing for the amount back or for it to be offset against the following rents.

**Also check these two points**

- The insurance premium can be charged to the tenant (art. 23, XI), but insurance **plus** a deposit or a guarantor would be two guarantees, which is prohibited (art. 37, sole paragraph).
- If the contract also charges a "contract" or "brokerage" fee to you, that is the landlord's cost (art. 22, VII).
