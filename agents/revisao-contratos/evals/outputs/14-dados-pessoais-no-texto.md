**Clause 5: guarantor + cash security deposit of 2 months' rent**

| Clause | What it says | Classification | Legal basis | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Guarantor and also a 2-month cash deposit | **ILLEGAL** | Law 8.245/1991, art. 37, sole paragraph; art. 43, II; art. 45 | Ask for a single guarantee |

**Why**

- The law allows **only one type of guarantee per contract**, on pain of nullity (art. 37, sole paragraph). A guarantor (surety) and a cash deposit are two types.
- Requiring more than one is a criminal misdemeanor (art. 43, II): simple imprisonment of 5 days to 6 months or a fine of 3 to 12 months' rent, paid to the tenant. The extra guarantee is void, and the tenant can ask for what was given in excess back.
- A 2-month deposit alone is within the legal cap of **3 months' rent**, and it must be held in a **savings account**, with the earnings going to the tenant (art. 38, par. 2). Two guarantors would still count as one type.

**What to do (assuming you are the tenant, is that right?)**

1. Choose one guarantee: the guarantor, the deposit, or rental guarantee insurance (art. 41).
2. Ask in writing for the clause to be corrected. Message:
   > "Hello, [name]. Clause 5 requires a guarantor and also a 2-month cash deposit. Under art. 37, sole paragraph, of Law 8.245/1991, only one type of guarantee is allowed. Can we keep only [guarantor / deposit]? Suggested wording: 'The lease is guaranteed exclusively by [type], under art. 37 of Law 8.245/1991.' Thank you."
3. If you keep the deposit, ask that it go to a savings account with earnings to you and for a deadline to return it after the move-out inspection.
4. Do not sign until the clause is fixed.
