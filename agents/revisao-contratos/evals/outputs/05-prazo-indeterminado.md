**Short answer: no penalty for leaving, as long as you give 30 days' written notice.**

Your 30-month written contract ended in March 2026 and you stayed in the property. Under Law 8.245/1991, art. 46, par. 1, the lease then continues for an **indefinite term** (with the same clauses). With no term left to serve, the early termination penalty of art. 4 **does not apply**.

You can leave at any time by giving **30 days' written notice** (art. 6).

**What happens if you skip the notice:** the landlord can charge **one month of rent and charges** at the current amounts (art. 6, sole paragraph).

**Important about "next month":** the 30 days run from the notice. If you give notice today and want to leave in less than 30 days, you could be charged the one-month amount for the shortfall. To avoid that, send the notice now and set the return date at least 30 days after it.

**What to do**

1. Send the notice **in writing**, with proof of delivery (e-mail with a reply, registered letter with return receipt or notice through a notary's office), and keep a copy:

   > "[City], [date]. Dear [name of landlord or agency]. I am writing to inform you that I will vacate the property at [address] on [date, at least 30 days ahead], under art. 6 of Law 8.245/1991. Please schedule the move-out inspection for [suggested date] and let me know the deadline for returning the security deposit, if any. Kind regards, [name]."

2. Ask for the **move-out inspection** with both parties present, with photos, and a signed key handover form. The guarantee usually lasts until the keys are actually returned (art. 39).
3. Compare with the move-in report, if there is one: you answer for damage you caused, not for normal wear and tear (art. 23, III and V).
4. If you left a **security deposit**, ask for it back with the savings account earnings (art. 38, par. 2), deducting only proven debts.
5. Keep paying the rent normally until you return the keys: non-payment is grounds for eviction (art. 9, III).
