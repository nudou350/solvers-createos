**Short answer: half of it. The IPTU yes, the facade painting no.**

| Item | Classification | Legal basis | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| IPTU paid by the tenant | **OK** (if written expressly) | Law 8.245/1991, art. 22, VIII | Say it expressly and state how it is billed (monthly or in installments) |
| Extra condominium fee for painting the facade | **ILLEGAL (void)** | art. 22, X and sole paragraph, item b; art. 45 | Keep it with the landlord |

**IPTU.** By law, property taxes and fees (IPTU, garbage fee) and the supplementary fire insurance are the landlord's, **unless the contract expressly says otherwise** (art. 22, VIII). Passing the IPTU to the tenant is allowed and common, as long as it is clear. A vague wording such as "charges in general" would be attention, not ok. Tip: confirm whether it comes in installments or a single payment and whether there are old debts (debts from before the lease are not the tenant's).

**Facade painting.** It is an **extraordinary** condominium expense, and extraordinary expenses are the landlord's (art. 22, X and sole paragraph, item b). Unlike the IPTU, item X has no "unless otherwise provided". A clause that shifts it to the tenant contradicts the law and is void (art. 45). The tenant pays only **ordinary** expenses (art. 23, XII): staff, water, electricity, cleaning, upkeep, elevators, small repairs and painting of internal common areas, and replenishing the reserve fund after ordinary use, with a budget forecast and monthly apportionment that can be proven (art. 23, par. 2). Extraordinary items also include structural works, installing security or leisure equipment and creating the reserve fund.

**Suggested wording**

> "The TENANT will pay the IPTU levied on the property, in addition to the ordinary condominium expenses (art. 23, XII of Law 8.245/1991). Extraordinary condominium expenses (art. 22, X) will be borne by the LANDLORD."

**Other points for you as the landlord**

- Keep one guarantee only. Requiring two (for example a guarantor and a deposit) is void and a misdemeanor (art. 37, sole paragraph; art. 43, II). A cash deposit cannot exceed 3 months' rent (art. 38, par. 2).
- Attach a move-in inspection report: without it, it is hard to charge for damage (art. 22, V; art. 23, III).
- Include a clause for validity if the property is sold (art. 8) if you want a fixed term respected by a buyer.
