**What the law provides (it does not predict the result)**

- The landlord can recover the property only through an **eviction action** (art. 5). Non-payment of rent and charges is grounds for ending the lease (art. 9, III).
- In an eviction for non-payment, the tenant (and the guarantor) can **avoid termination by paying, within 15 days from the summons, the updated debt as a court deposit** (art. 62, II). It includes:
  - rent and charges due up to the deposit;
  - enforceable contractual fines;
  - default interest;
  - court costs and the landlord's lawyer's **10% fees**, if the contract does not set another amount.
- If the landlord says the deposit was too low, you can **top it up within 10 days** (art. 62, III).
- Rent that falls due during the case must also be deposited in court (art. 62, V).
- **Limit:** you cannot use this option if you already did within the last 24 months before the action (art. 62, sole paragraph).
- If the contract has **no guarantee at all**, non-payment allows an injunction to vacate in 15 days, which you avoid by depositing everything you owe within the deadline (art. 59, par. 1, IX and par. 3).

6. Tell the lawyer about any guarantor: they may also be called to pay.
