**Attention: your case has high-risk signals (three months of overdue rent and a threat of eviction). Seek a lawyer or the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública) before replying to the landlord. If you are served with a summons, the 15-day period to pay and avoid eviction runs from the summons (art. 62, II of Law 8.245/1991).**

I cannot guarantee that you will win, and nobody honestly can. Please do not count on it: non-payment of rent and charges is grounds for ending the lease (art. 9, III), so going to court without paying is risky. What I can tell you is what the law provides.

**What the law provides**

- The landlord **cannot evict you on his own**. Only an eviction action takes the property back (art. 5). Changing the lock, cutting off water or electricity or removing your belongings is not allowed.
- If he files an eviction action for non-payment, you (and the guarantor) can **avoid termination** by depositing the updated debt in court **within 15 days from the summons** (art. 62, II): rent and charges due, enforceable contractual fines, interest, court costs and 10% lawyer's fees if the contract sets no other amount. If the deposit is short, you can top it up within 10 days (art. 62, III), and rent that falls due during the case must be deposited too (art. 62, V).
- You **cannot** use that option if you already did in the 24 months before the action (art. 62, sole paragraph).

**What tends to help (not a promise)**

1. **Talk to the Defensoria Pública or a lawyer now**, with the contract and your payment proofs. Only a professional can check whether there is a real defense (for example, defects in the property or an error in the amount charged).
2. **Add up the debt** (3 months' rent + charges + contractual fine + interest + 10% fees) so you know what it would cost to cure the default.
3. **Propose a written payment plan** to the landlord and keep the reply. Pay what you can and always keep proof.
4. **Do not ignore any court document** and do not move out of your own accord without advice.
5. **Keep paying the rent that falls due** from now on.
