I'd advise against it, and I'll show you the numbers rather than just say no. A promise of 3% a month with no risk is a strong warning sign.

**Why this is a red flag**

- **No investment guarantees a return with no risk at that level.** In the real market, the more an investment promises, the more risk it carries. A guaranteed high fixed return is the classic pattern of a pyramid or Ponzi-type scheme: the early payouts come from new people's money.
- **The math:** 3% a month compounds to about 42.6% a year (1.03 to the 12th power). Today the Selic is 13.75% a year (Copom, September 16, 2026) and the CDI is near 13.65%, which is about 1.1% a month before tax. The group would be promising roughly 3 times what the safest floating-rate products in the country pay, with no risk. That doesn't add up.
- **There is no FGC or other guarantee behind it.** The FGC covers specific products at member institutions (CDB, LCI, LCA, poupança and so on, up to R$ 250,000 per person and institution), not a promise made by a group. If the group disappears, there is usually no one to claim the money from.

**Why it is worse for an emergency fund**

An emergency fund must be safe and available the same day. Money in an investment like this can be lost entirely and be locked up when you need it. In a bad moment, that is the money that keeps you from getting into debt.

**What I suggest**

1. Don't put your emergency fund, or money you can't afford to lose, in it. Don't pay any "entry fee" or give your passwords, CPF or documents to the group.
2. Be suspicious of pressure ("only today", "vacancies are limited") and of paying rewards for bringing in friends.
3. Check whether the offer is made by an authorized institution and whether you can find complaints about it. When in doubt, ask a certified, independent professional.
4. If you've already put money in, keep the proof of payment and conversations and consider reporting it to the consumer and authority channels (Procon, consumidor.gov.br, police).

If you want a safe place for the fund, categories like floating-rate government bonds, a daily-liquidity CDB covered by the FGC or a remunerated account fit (about 10.6% a year net within 180 days at 100% of the CDI, against about 8.2% for poupança). These are references, not product recommendations.

Two questions:

1. Have you already put money into this group? How much?
2. How much do you have in your fund today and what are your monthly essential costs?
