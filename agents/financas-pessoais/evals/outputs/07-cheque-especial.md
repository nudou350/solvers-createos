Yes, it is very expensive, and using it almost every month is a sign worth looking at calmly, with no judgment.

**The math**

- Overdraft (cheque especial) interest is capped at 8% a month for individuals and MEIs (Resolução CMN 4.765/2019, in force since January 2020).
- R$ 1,500 used for a whole month costs up to **R$ 120 in interest** (1,500 x 8%).
- In a year of constant use, that is over R$ 1,400 in interest alone (12 x R$ 120 = R$ 1,440), and you still owe the R$ 1,500.
- Your real rate is on your account statement, so check whether it is below the cap. The 8% a month is about 152% a year.

For comparison, it is below the average for revolving card credit (about 15% a month in August 2026), but still very expensive. Overdraft is meant for emergencies lasting a few days, not for topping up the whole salary every month.

**What I suggest**

1. **Swap it for cheaper installment debt.** Compare the CET (total effective cost) of an installment loan, credit portability or a payroll loan (if you have access) with the overdraft cost. The swap pays off only if the new installment fits the budget.
2. **Lower the overdraft limit** (you can ask the bank, usually in the app) so you don't go back to it. Without that, you end up with two debts.
3. **Look at why it happens every month.** If you dip into overdraft every month, usually there is a monthly gap between income and spending. Then the plan starts by closing that gap, not by saving. A diagnosis of where the money goes (essentials, variable spending, installments) is the next step.

Up to three questions so I can build this with your numbers:

1. How much do you net per month, and is it fixed or does it vary?
2. What is your rate on the overdraft (on the statement or in the app) and do you have other debts (card, loans)?
3. Are you formally employed or retired (to see if a payroll loan is an option), or self-employed or informal?

Rules and rates change: check them at bcb.gov.br and on your contract.
