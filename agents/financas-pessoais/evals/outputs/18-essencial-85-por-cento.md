No, not as it is. The 50/30/20 rule is a starting point, not a law, and it only works when the essentials fit in about half of the income. Yours don't, and forcing the rule would just produce guilt.

**Your numbers**

| Item | R$ | % of income |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | 2,000 | 100% |
| Essentials (rent, food, transport) | 1,700 | **85%** |
| What's left | 300 | 15% |

**What the method says by essentials band**

- Up to 50%: 50/30/20.
- 50% to 60%: 60/20/20.
- 60% to 70%: a temporary 70/20/10.
- **Above 70%: the problem is income or fixed costs, not discipline.** The split rules don't apply: first you discuss structural cuts or more income. Hence, no "wants" and "future" percentages to apply yet.

You have R$ 300 a month left, and everything else (utility bills? health? debts? leisure?) has to come out of it. That is tight, and it is not a personal failing.

**Where I'd start**

1. **Break down the R$ 1,700.** Which item is the biggest, and can any of it change (a cheaper rent, a shared home, transport costs, a plan or tariff)? Small cuts in a large item beat many small cuts.
2. **Look at the income side**: any extra income, even small, changes the picture more than cuts do at this level.
3. **Check for debts.** If revolving credit or overdraft is in the mix, it comes first, because it costs more than anything else.
4. **Start a tiny fund anyway**: once the picture is confirmed, an automatic transfer on payday (even R$ 50 to R$ 100) builds the first reserve. If there is expensive debt, the reserve stays minimal until it is paid off.
5. If installments take most of what's left and the basics fall short, over-indebtedness help (Procon, Defensoria) is an option.

Up to three questions:

1. Does the R$ 1,700 include any debt installments? Do you have any debts?
2. Is your income fixed or does it vary, and does anyone depend only on you?
3. How much is the biggest item (usually rent) and how much do utility bills, health and everything else come to?
