This is financial education, not professional financial advice or an investment recommendation.

I won't name a bank, a specific CDB or "the best one", and I want to explain why that is in your favor: a "best one" depends on your situation, changes every week and I can't verify the real conditions. What I can do is give you the criteria to choose and check on your own.

**The three criteria, in this order**

1. **Safety**: the risk of losing the money.
2. **Liquidity**: can you redeem it the same day or the next business day? An emergency fund needs this.
3. **Yield**: only after the first two.

**Categories that usually fit an emergency fund**

- A floating-rate government bond (the category of Tesouro Selic): federal government risk, redemption on a business day. It does not depend on the FGC, because the guarantee is the National Treasury.
- A floating-rate CDB or RDB with daily liquidity, near 100% of the CDI, at an institution covered by the FGC.
- A remunerated account that yields a percentage of the CDI (check whether the balance sits in a product covered by the FGC).
- Poupança: yields less today, but it is simple and exempt from income tax.

**Checklist when you compare any offer**

- Does it have daily liquidity (redemption the same day or next day, with no lock-up)?
- Is the institution covered by the FGC? The limit is R$ 250,000 per person and institution or conglomerate (R$ 1 million overall every 4 years).
- What is the yield as a % of the CDI, and what is it net of income tax? (22.5% up to 180 days, 20% up to 360, 17.5% up to 720, 15% beyond; IOF if redeemed before 30 days.)
- Is the rate far above the market (for example, 130% of the CDI)? That usually signals a riskier institution, and an FGC payout can take weeks.

With the Selic at 13.75% (Copom, September 16, 2026), a floating-rate investment with daily liquidity near 100% of the CDI yields about 10.6% a year net in the first 6 months, against about 8.2% for poupança. Those are reference figures, not an endorsement of any product.

To help you size it, up to three questions:

1. What are your monthly essential costs and is your income fixed or variable? (That sets the fund: 3 to 6 months or 6 to 12.)
2. How much do you have today?
3. Do you have revolving card credit or overdraft? If so, paying that off comes before building the full fund.

Check the rates and the FGC limits at bcb.gov.br, fgc.org.br and tesourodireto.com.br.
