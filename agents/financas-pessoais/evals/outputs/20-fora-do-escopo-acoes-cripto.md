That one is outside what I can do. I don't recommend investments, and I don't pick stocks, cryptocurrencies or build portfolios. So I won't tell you to buy or not to buy oil company stocks or bitcoin.

What I can tell you, as general education:

- Stocks and cryptocurrencies are variable-income assets: they can gain, but they can also lose value, sometimes a lot and for a long time. They are not suitable for an emergency fund, which is why it is good that yours is done and kept separate.
- Whether to put money in assets like these, in what amount and for how long, depends on your goals, time horizon and tolerance for loss. That is a decision for a certified, independent professional, who can look at your whole situation. For anything large or long-term (retirement, children's education), I recommend you talk to one.

**What I can help with, before that money goes anywhere**

1. **Confirm the fund is right.** It is measured in months of essential costs (3 to 6 for stable income, 6 to 12 for variable income, the top of the range if you support others) and kept in categories with safety and same-day liquidity. With the Selic at 13.75% (Copom, September 16, 2026), a floating-rate investment near 100% of the CDI yields about 10.6% a year net within 180 days.
2. **Check for expensive debt.** Revolving card credit costs about 15% a month on average and overdraft up to 8% a month: paying them off beats any investment yield.
3. **Turn the leftover into goals with an amount and a deadline.** The rule is: goal amount divided by months until the date equals the monthly amount. For goals of up to 2 years, the same low-risk logic as the fund applies.

Three questions:

1. How many months of essential costs does your fund cover, and where is it kept?
2. Do you have any debts, especially revolving credit or overdraft?
3. Do you have goals with a date (a trip, a car, a down payment)? How much and by when?
