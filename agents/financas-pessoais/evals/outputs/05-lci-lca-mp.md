What you heard was a proposal that did not become law, as far as I could verify.

**What happened with the 5%**

- In June 2025 the government issued Medida Provisória 1.303/2025, which provided for 5% income tax on earnings from LCI, LCA and other currently exempt securities issued from 2026 on.
- The Chamber of Deputies pulled the MP from the agenda on October 8, 2025 and it lapsed (caducou) without becoming law.
- As of October 2026, LCI and LCA remain exempt from income tax for individuals, and the regressive table for CDB and Tesouro (22.5% / 20% / 17.5% / 15%) is still in force.
- The government returned to the topic in 2026, but I found no new law in my knowledge base. Please check with the Receita Federal (gov.br/receitafederal) and planalto.gov.br whether something new was approved, because this can change.

**Is it worth it? Compare net with net**

With the Selic at 13.75% (Copom, September 16, 2026) and the CDI near 13.65% a year, a rough yearly simulation:

| Category | Gross | Income tax | Approx. net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poupança | ~8.2% | exempt | ~8.2% |
| Floating-rate at 100% of the CDI, redeemed within 180 days | ~13.65% | 22.5% | ~10.6% |
| Floating-rate at 100% of the CDI, after 720 days | ~13.65% | 15% | ~11.6% |
| LCI/LCA at 90% of the CDI | ~12.3% | exempt | ~12.3% |

So yes, an exempt note can yield more net than a taxed one, but exempt does not mean better. The catch: LCI and LCA usually have a lock-up period (no redemption). That is a poor fit for an emergency fund, which needs same-day redemption, and a better fit for money you won't touch for a known period. I am not recommending any product; this is the math to explain the trade-off.

Two questions so I can help more precisely:

1. Is this money for your emergency fund or for a goal with a date? For how long can you leave it untouched?
2. Do you have any expensive debt (revolving card credit, overdraft)? If so, paying it off beats any investment yield.
