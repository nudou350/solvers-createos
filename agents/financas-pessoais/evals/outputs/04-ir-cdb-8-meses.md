This is financial education, not professional financial advice or an investment recommendation. This is Brazilian tax as of October 2026.

For a CDB held about 8 months (about 240 days), the income tax rate is **20% on the earnings**, not on the amount you invest.

**Regressive income tax table (Lei 11.033/2004)**

| Investment term | Income tax on earnings |
|---|---|
| Up to 180 days | 22.5% |
| 181 to 360 days | **20%** |
| 361 to 720 days | 17.5% |
| Over 720 days | 15% |

**Example with made-up numbers:** R$ 10,000 invested for 240 days earns R$ 800. The tax is 20% of R$ 800 = **R$ 160**. You receive R$ 10,640. The amount you invested is never taxed.

Things to watch:

- The term counts per investment. If you make deposits along the way, each deposit has its own clock.
- It is withheld at the source on redemption: the bank already pays the tax, you receive the net amount.
- If you redeem before 30 days, you also pay IOF on the earnings (96% on day 1, falling to 0% from day 30). The amount you invested comes back in full.
- If you redeem before 180 days, the rate would be 22.5%. So the exact day counts: with "about 8 months" you are comfortably in the 20% band, but not by a margin of weeks if you redeem much earlier.
- Compare products net with net. Poupança, LCI and LCA are exempt for individuals, but exempt does not always mean better, and LCI/LCA usually have a lock-up period.
- CDB earnings go into your annual income tax return if you are required to file; the bank sends the income statement (informe de rendimentos). For tax questions, an accountant is the right person.

Taxes on investments have been the target of proposals in 2025 and 2026, so the rule may change: confirm with the Receita Federal (gov.br/receitafederal) and planalto.gov.br.
