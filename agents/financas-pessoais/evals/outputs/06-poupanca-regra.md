It depends on what you value. Today poupança is simple, but it usually yields less than other low-risk options.

**How poupança yields (Lei 12.703/2012, deposits since May 4, 2012)**

| Selic | Poupança yield |
|---|---|
| Above 8.5% a year | 0.5% a month + TR |
| Equal to or below 8.5% a year | 70% of the Selic + TR |

With the Selic at 13.75% (Copom, September 16, 2026), the first row applies: 0.5% a month + TR (the TR, reference rate, is around 0.17% a month and changes monthly). That gives about 0.66% to 0.67% a month, close to 8.2% a year. Poupança is exempt from income tax for individuals, has no IOF and is covered by the FGC (R$ 250,000 per person and institution or conglomerate). The yield is credited only on the deposit's "anniversary": withdrawing before the day loses that month's yield.

**Rough comparison (yearly, net)**

| Category | Approx. net |
|---|---|
| Poupança | ~8.2% |
| Floating-rate at about 100% of the CDI, redeemed within 180 days | ~10.6% |
| Floating-rate at about 100% of the CDI, after 720 days | ~11.6% |

With the Selic above 8.5%, a floating-rate investment with daily liquidity near 100% of the CDI usually yields more than poupança even after income tax. Poupança still works for someone who wants total simplicity. This is the math, not a recommendation of a product. The rates change at every Copom meeting (the next is November 3 and 4, 2026), and if the Selic falls to 8.5% or below, the poupança rule changes.

Also remember: if you have revolving card credit or overdraft, paying it off beats any savings yield. For example, R$ 2,000 in poupança earns about R$ 13 a month, while R$ 2,000 in revolving credit costs about R$ 300.

To say something useful for your case:

1. Is this money your emergency fund or for a goal with a date?
2. Do you have any debts, especially revolving credit or overdraft?
3. Do you need to withdraw it the same day if something happens?
