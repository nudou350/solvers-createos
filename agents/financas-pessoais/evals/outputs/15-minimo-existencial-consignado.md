Two parts, two answers.

**1. Does the payroll loan count? According to the reported decision, yes.**

On April 23, 2026, the STF (Brazil's Supreme Federal Court) concluded its judgment on the existential minimum (mínimo existencial). According to the reported decision, payroll-deducted loan (consignado) debts now also count toward it. I'm describing the news reports, so check the decision's text or ask Procon/Defensoria how it applies in your case.

In practice: the existential minimum is the part of your income that must be left after consumer installments. In a restructuring under Lei 14.181/2021, the plan cannot leave you with less than that.

**2. Is R$ 600 valid forever? No.**

- Decreto 11.150/2022 set it at R$ 303. Decreto 11.567/2023 raised it to **R$ 600**, the amount in force in October 2026.
- The STF kept the R$ 600 parameter and ordered the National Monetary Council (CMN) to **review the amount at least once a year**. Until the review, R$ 600 applies, so check whether it has been updated.

**What this means for you**

You have three kinds of debts (payroll loan, card, personal loan). If together the installments eat so much of your income that you can't cover the basics, this is over-indebtedness (superendividamento). As a rule of thumb in this method (not a criterion in the law), installments above 30% to 40% of income with not enough left for the basics are a signal.

Asking for help is not defaulting. You can request a block restructuring (repactuação): a conciliation hearing with all creditors at once, with a payment plan of up to 5 years, preserving the existential minimum. Where: Procon, the Defensoria or your court's over-indebtedness center (CEJUSC). Debts with a security interest, real estate financing, rural credit, child support and taxes are left out.

Up to three questions so we can look at your numbers:

1. What is your net monthly income, and what do the basics (housing, food, transport, health) cost?
2. What is the installment of each of the three debts, and the balance and rate of each, if you know?
3. Are any of them overdue, and since when?

Rules and amounts can change: check planalto.gov.br and the CMN/Banco Central website.
