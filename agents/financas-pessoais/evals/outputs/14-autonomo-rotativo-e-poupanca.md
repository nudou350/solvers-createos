Short answer: in most cases, use the poupança to pay off the card, keeping a small reserve, because the debt costs far more than the savings earn. With variable income, though, how much you keep matters, so let's do it carefully.

**The math (estimated, using Central Bank averages)**

- R$ 4,000 in revolving credit at about 15% a month (August 2026 average, 444.9% a year) costs about **R$ 600 in interest per month**. Your real rate is on your bill.
- R$ 10,000 in poupança, with the Selic at 13.75% (Copom, September 16, 2026), earns 0.5% + TR, about 0.67% a month: **about R$ 67 a month**.
- So you earn roughly R$ 67 while paying roughly R$ 600. Paying off the debt is the best "investment" here.
- Also remember the rule: the balance can stay in revolving credit only until the next bill (about 30 days), then it becomes an installment plan. The cap of 100% of the original amount (Lei 14.690/2023) limits the damage but doesn't make it cheap.

**How I'd split it**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Poupança today | R$ 10,000 |
| Pay off the card in full | R$ 4,000 |
| Left in the fund | R$ 6,000 |

Why it works: the method says to keep only a minimum reserve (R$ 500 to R$ 1,000, or half a month of essentials) while there is expensive debt. R$ 6,000 is well above that. A caveat: if your essentials are high, the R$ 6,000 matters more, so look at the worst month. If you pay the card with the poupança and still have the minimum reserve, don't keep the debt "just in case".

**Emergency fund after the card**

With income that varies, the target is 6 to 12 months of essential costs (not income). If, for example, your essentials were R$ 3,000, the target would be R$ 18,000 to R$ 36,000, and R$ 6,000 would be 2 months. The plan would then be: rebuild the fund with a monthly amount calculated on your worst month (about R$ 3,000), and add more in good months.

The golden rule: pay the card off and stop using it for things outside the budget. Otherwise you spend the savings and the debt comes back. Pause the card until the plan stabilizes.

Three questions to confirm:

1. What do you need to cover each month (rent, food, transport, health, installments you can't stop paying)?
2. Do you have other debts besides the R$ 4,000 on the card?
3. Does anyone depend only on your money?

Check the rate on your bill and the numbers at bcb.gov.br.
