Short answer: no, you can't leave it in revolving credit for three months. And the reason matters, because it changes the plan.

**How it works in Brazil (Resolução CMN 4.549/2017, checked as of October 2026)**

- When you pay less than the full bill, the unpaid balance goes to revolving credit (crédito rotativo).
- It can stay there only until the due date of the next bill, about 30 days. After that the bank must turn it into an installment plan (parcelamento) for the balance, or you pay it off.
- So "revolving credit for three months" isn't an option. In one month the R$ 1,200 becomes an installment plan, usually also expensive.

**What it costs (estimated math)**

- Interest for the month is roughly balance x monthly rate. The Central Bank average for revolving credit in August 2026 was 444.9% a year, about 15% a month. That is an average: your real rate is on your bill.
- R$ 1,200 x 15% = about R$ 180 in interest in the first month. The installment plan averaged 192.1% a year (about 9.3% a month), so it is cheaper than revolving credit but still expensive.
- Lei 14.690/2023 caps interest and charges on revolving credit and on the card installment plan at 100% of the original amount. On R$ 1,200, the debt can reach at most R$ 2,400. This cap limits the damage; it does not make the debt cheap. At 15% a month, a debt doubles in about 5 months.

**What I suggest, in order**

1. Pay as much of the R$ 1,200 as you can now, before the next bill. Every real paid now stops generating interest.
2. Don't pay only the minimum: the rest goes to revolving credit, the most expensive path.
3. For what is left, ask the bank in writing for the installment amount, number of installments and the CET (total effective cost), and compare it with a personal loan or a payroll loan, if you have access. The swap only pays off if the new installment fits and you stop using the card for new spending.
4. Pause the card until the plan stabilizes.

To tune this to your case, I need up to three things:

1. What is the monthly interest rate or the CET on your bill? (If you don't know, I'll use the average above and mark it as "estimated".)
2. How much can you pay now, and how much can go to this debt per month without running short of the basics?
3. Do you have other debts (overdraft, loans) or savings that earn less than this debt costs?

Rates and rules change: check the rate on your bill and the rules at bcb.gov.br and planalto.gov.br.
