I'm sorry you're in this spot. First, there is a legal way out, and asking for help is not defaulting. What you describe (installments above half of your salary and not enough left for food) is the textbook case the over-indebtedness law was written for.

**What the law says (Lei 14.181/2021, Brazil)**

- Over-indebtedness (superendividamento) is when an individual, in good faith, cannot pay all consumer debts without compromising the **existential minimum** (mínimo existencial).
- You can ask for a **block restructuring (repactuação)**: a conciliation hearing with all creditors at the same time, with a payment plan of up to 5 years, preserving the existential minimum. A creditor that fails to show up without justification can have its collection suspended. Without an agreement, a judge can impose a compulsory plan.
- Where to ask: **Procon**, the **Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública)** or your court's over-indebtedness center (**CEJUSC**). It is free to ask at those.
- Existential minimum: R$ 600 (Decreto 11.567/2023). The STF concluded its judgment on April 23, 2026, kept R$ 600 and ordered the CMN to review it at least once a year; please check whether it has been updated. According to the reported decision, payroll-deducted loans also count. The 30% to 40% of income figure I use is a rule of thumb of this method, not a criterion in the law.
- Debts with a security interest, real estate financing, rural credit, child support, taxes and debts incurred through fraud are left out.

**What to do this week**

1. **Protect the basics first**: food, rent, electricity, water, medicine. Bills that cut off an essential service or risk an essential asset come before others, even with lower interest.
2. **List every debt** on one page: creditor, balance, installment, rate or CET, arrears. If you don't know what you owe, Registrato (a free Central Bank service, via your gov.br login) shows the credit operations in your name.
3. **Don't take a new loan to pay another one**, and don't pay any "fee" to someone who promises to clean your name. Be wary of anyone asking for your passwords.
4. If creditors harass or threaten you, that is banned by the Consumer Defense Code: complain at consumidor.gov.br and Procon.
5. Go to Procon, the Defensoria or CEJUSC with the list from step 2.

To work out your numbers together, up to three questions:

1. How much lands in your account each month, after deductions, and what do the basics (food, housing, transport, health) cost?
2. What are the debts (loan, card, others): balance, installment and, if you know, the rate? Are any overdue and since when?
3. Does anyone depend only on your money?

Check the amounts and rules at planalto.gov.br and with Procon before acting.
