I wouldn't put the whole emergency fund there, and the reason is the rate itself. I can't judge any specific bank, but I can show you how to read an offer like this.

**What 130% of the CDI means**

- With the CDI near 13.65% a year (Selic at 13.75%), 130% of the CDI is about 17.7% a year gross.
- Net of income tax within 180 days (22.5%), that is about 13.8% a year, against about 10.6% for a 100%-of-CDI floating-rate investment with daily liquidity, and about 8.2% for poupança.
- It looks like an extra 3 points a year. Whether it is worth it depends on the risk you take for it.

**Why caution**

- A rate far above the market, such as 130% of the CDI on daily liquidity, usually signals a riskier institution: it is paying more because it needs the funding more.
- The FGC covers up to R$ 250,000 per person and institution or conglomerate (R$ 1 million overall every 4 years), principal and earnings. Under that limit, the guarantee exists, but **payment can take weeks**. An emergency fund needs the money fast, so the real risk is not only losing it: it is losing access to it when you need it.
- The guarantee doesn't make any rate safe. Check whether the bank belongs to a conglomerate that you already use, because it shares the same limit.
- Fund criteria, in order: safety, liquidity, yield. This offer wins only on the third.

**What I suggest**

- Don't concentrate the whole fund in one place, least of all in the highest-yield, highest-risk option. Keep the core of the fund in the lowest-risk categories with same-day liquidity (floating-rate government bond, a daily-liquidity CDB from an institution covered by the FGC near 100% of the CDI, remunerated account, poupança).
- If you still want to use something like this, treat it as a small share of money that can wait weeks, and confirm in writing the daily-liquidity terms (are there minimum terms? what is the redemption timing?).
- Compare everything net of tax and IOF, and check the institution's situation on official channels. A certified, independent professional can help you evaluate a specific institution.

Three questions to help:

1. How much is the fund today, and how many months of essentials is it?
2. Where is the money now, and can you withdraw it the same day?
3. Do you have any expensive debt (revolving credit, overdraft)?

Rates and limits change: confirm at bcb.gov.br and fgc.org.br.
