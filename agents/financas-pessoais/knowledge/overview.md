# Personal finance: method overview

This specialist organizes day-to-day finances. It is financial education: it is not professional financial advice, it is not an investment recommendation and it does not name specific banks, brokers, funds or bonds. The numbers and rules that change (Selic, taxes, FGC, interest caps, renegotiation programs) live in the other files of the knowledge base, each with a source and a validity date. If the text warns that the passage may be out of date, tell the user and ask them to confirm at the official source.

This package is Brazil-specific: the rules, rates, taxes and programs described here apply to people living in Brazil and to credit and investments contracted in Brazil (amounts in Brazilian reais, R$). The budgeting method itself (diagnosis, debt order, emergency fund, monthly plan) works anywhere, but the legal and tax checks do not. If the user lives elsewhere, say so up front.

## Diagnosis before rules

Almost everyone underestimates variable spending (usually by 20% to 30%). Work with real numbers, even approximate ones:

- **Net income**: what lands in the account, separating fixed from variable. For self-employed people, use the average of the last 6 months and the worst month.
- **Fixed expenses**: housing, utility bills, school, transport, installments.
- **Variable expenses**: groceries, delivery, leisure, shopping. The last 3 statements and card bills help.
- **Debts**: balance, installment, monthly rate and **CET** (custo efetivo total, the total effective cost: interest, fees, insurance and taxes). The CET is the right number for comparing two debts.
- **Money saved**: how much and where.

Anyone who does not know what they owe can check Registrato, a free Central Bank of Brazil service that shows the credit operations in the person's name. Access is through the gov.br login, and the access rules change: check the Central Bank's website.

## Debt attack order

Prioritize by cost, not by size. From the most expensive to the cheapest:

1. **Revolving credit card debt**: the most expensive in the market.
2. **Overdraft (cheque especial)**: capped at 8% per month, still very expensive.
3. **Card bill installment plans and unsecured personal loans.**
4. **Payroll-deducted loans (consignado) and secured financing**: usually the cheapest.

A common-sense exception: bills that cut off an essential service or lead to losing an asset (rent, electricity, water, child support, financing on a vehicle used for work) come first, even with lower interest. The **avalanche** method (highest rate first) saves the most interest; the **snowball** method (smallest balance first) gives quick wins. Explain the difference and let the person choose.

## Budget split: adapted 50/30/20

The 50/30/20 rule (needs, wants, future) is a starting point, not a law. In Brazil, essentials easily pass 60% of income. Adapt it by the essentials percentage:

- Up to 50%: **50/30/20**.
- From 50% to 60%: **60/20/20**.
- From 60% to 70%: **70/20/10**, temporary, with a goal of returning to the previous band.
- Above 70%: the problem is income or fixed costs (rent, car, health plan). Discuss structural cuts or more income before any rule.
- With expensive debt: the "future" share goes to paying off the debt, keeping only a minimum reserve (R$ 500 to R$ 1,000, or half a month of essentials).
- Pay yourself first: transfer the future share on the day the money comes in.

## Habits that sustain the plan

- A 10-minute weekly review is worth more than a perfect spreadsheet.
- Weekly caps for variable spending work better than monthly ones (the month's amount divided by 4.3).
- Automate fixed bills and the transfer to the emergency fund.
- Cancel forgotten subscriptions: check the bills of the last 3 months.
- Use the card only for what was already in the budget. If the bill exceeds the month's income, pause the card until the plan stabilizes.

## When to refer to outside help

- **Over-indebtedness** (installments of 30% to 40% of income or more, with a risk of running short of the basics): Procon, the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública) or the court's over-indebtedness center.
- **Abusive charges or a bank error**: the institution's customer service (SAC) and ombudsman, then consumidor.gov.br and the Central Bank.
- **Big investment decisions** (inheritance, selling a property, private pension, portfolio): a certified, independent professional. This specialist does not build portfolios.
