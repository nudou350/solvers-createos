# Debt renegotiation and over-indebtedness in Brazil

Financial education, not professional legal advice. This file describes Brazilian law (Código de Defesa do Consumidor, as amended by Lei 14.181/2021). For decisions about a lawsuit, see the Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública), Procon or a lawyer. The amount of the existential minimum may be updated at any time: ask the user to check whether it has changed.

## Renegotiating directly with the creditor

1. **List the debts** with balance, arrears and rate (debt table template). Registrato, from the Central Bank, shows the credit operations in the person's name; access is through the gov.br login and the rules change.
2. **Decide how much fits each month** before calling. Never accept an installment that does not fit just to "clean your name".
3. **Ask for an up-front offer and an installment offer.** An old debt often gets a large discount when paid up front.
4. **Compare by total amount and by CET**, not just by the installment.
5. **Demand everything in writing** (the agreement document and the payment slip) and keep the receipt.
6. After payment, the creditor has **5 business days** to request removal of the name from the delinquent-debtor registries (Súmula 548 of the STJ, the Superior Court of Justice). Check afterwards.

An old negative record leaves the registries after **5 years**, but the debt can still be collected by other means: do not build a plan on waiting for it to "expire".

## Over-indebtedness: Lei 14.181/2021

The law amended the Consumer Defense Code (Código de Defesa do Consumidor). Over-indebtedness (superendividamento) is the inability of an individual, in good faith, to pay all of their consumer debts without compromising the **existential minimum** (mínimo existencial).

- **Block restructuring (repactuação)**: a conciliation hearing with **all creditors at the same time**, with a payment plan of up to **5 years**, preserving the existential minimum.
- Where to ask: **Procon**, the **Public Defender's Office (Defensoria Pública)** and the **courts' over-indebtedness centers** (CEJUSC). Without an agreement, the judge can impose a compulsory plan.
- A creditor that fails to show up without justification may have its collection suspended and be paid only after the others.
- The law also bans credit advertising with "no SPC/Serasa check" and harassment to sign up for credit, especially of the elderly.

Left out of the restructuring: debts with a security interest (garantia real), real estate financing, rural credit, child support, taxes and debts incurred through fraud or bad faith.

## Existential minimum: R$ 600 and the Supreme Court ruling

- Decreto 11.150/2022 set the existential minimum at R$ 303. **Decreto 11.567/2023** raised it to **R$ 600**, the amount in force in October 2026.
- On **April 23, 2026** the STF (Brazil's Supreme Federal Court) concluded its judgment: it kept the R$ 600 parameter and ordered the **National Monetary Council (CMN) to review the amount at least once a year**. Until the review, R$ 600 applies. **Check whether it has been updated.**
- According to the reported decision, **payroll-deducted loan (consignado)** debts now also count toward the existential minimum.
- The existential minimum is the part of income that must be left after consumer installments. In a restructuring, the plan cannot leave the person with less than that.

## Signs to refer someone to over-indebtedness help

Refer them to Procon, the Public Defender's Office, CEJUSC or consumidor.gov.br when:

- Installments pass **30% to 40% of income** and there is not enough money for food, rent or medicine.
- The person takes out a loan to pay another one every month.
- There are several creditors and no individual renegotiation fits the budget.
- There is abusive collection, threats or embarrassment (banned by the Consumer Defense Code).

Explain in plain language that **asking for a restructuring is not defaulting**: it is a right provided by law for those acting in good faith. The 30% to 40% figures are a rule of thumb of this method, not a criterion in the law.
