# Interest and yields in Brazil: Selic, CDI and poupança

Financial education, not an investment recommendation. Numbers as of October 2, 2026, valid until the next Copom decision (November 4, 2026). The Selic changes at every meeting (8 a year): never present these values as permanent, and ask the user to check bcb.gov.br.

## The Selic rate in September 2026

- **Selic target: 13.75% a year**, set by the Copom (the Central Bank's monetary policy committee) on September 16, 2026 (a cut of 0.25 point, from 14.00%).
- It was the fifth cut in a row since March 2026 (15.00% in January, then 14.75%, 14.50%, 14.25%, 14.00% and 13.75%).
- Next meetings: November 3 and 4, 2026 and December 8 and 9, 2026 (calendar on the Central Bank's website).

The Selic is the benchmark interest rate. It drives the yield of floating-rate investments (Tesouro Selic, CDI-linked CDBs, remunerated accounts) and, indirectly, the cost of loans.

## CDI: what it is and where it stands

The **CDI** is the rate on overnight loans between banks and tracks the Selic closely, about 0.10 point below. With the target at 13.75%, the CDI is near **13.65% a year** (reference of September 25, 2026; the day's value is on the B3 and Central Bank websites).

"100% of the CDI" yields the same as the CDI **before income tax**; "110% of the CDI" yields 10% more than that. Always compare the **net** yield (after income tax) with that of poupança, which is exempt. The income tax table is in the fixed-income taxes file.

## Poupança: yield rule

Lei 12.703/2012, for deposits made on or after May 4, 2012:

| Selic situation | Poupança yield |
|---|---|
| Above 8.5% a year | 0.5% a month + TR |
| Equal to or below 8.5% a year | 70% of the Selic + TR |

- With the Selic at 13.75%, the first row applies: 0.5% a month + TR.
- The TR (taxa referencial, the reference rate) is around 0.17% a month, which gives about **0.66% to 0.67% a month, or close to 8.2% a year**. The TR changes every month.
- The yield is credited only on the deposit's "anniversary": withdrawing before the day loses that month's yield.
- Poupança is exempt from income tax for individuals, has no IOF and is covered by the FGC (rules in the emergency fund and FGC file).

## Poupança or floating-rate: rough comparison

Yearly simulation, with the Selic at 13.75% and the CDI at about 13.65%. It is meant to explain the math, not to choose a product.

| Investment (category) | Gross yield | Income tax | Approximate net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poupança | ~8.2% | exempt | ~8.2% |
| Floating-rate at 100% of the CDI, redeemed within 180 days | ~13.65% | 22.5% of earnings | ~10.6% |
| Floating-rate at 100% of the CDI, after 720 days | ~13.65% | 15% of earnings | ~11.6% |
| LCI/LCA at 90% of the CDI (exempt for individuals) | ~12.3% | exempt | ~12.3% |

With the Selic above 8.5%, a floating-rate investment with daily liquidity near 100% of the CDI usually yields more than poupança even after income tax. LCI and LCA usually have a lock-up period (no redemption) and do not always suit an emergency fund. Poupança still works for people who want total simplicity.

## Debt interest versus investment yield

If the debt charges more than the investment earns, paying off the debt is the best "investment". With September 2026 numbers:

- R$ 2,000 in poupança earns about **R$ 13 a month** (0.66% to 0.67%).
- R$ 2,000 in revolving card credit costs about **R$ 300 a month** (an average of around 15% a month in August 2026, according to the Central Bank).
- R$ 2,000 in overdraft costs up to **R$ 160 a month** (cap of 8% a month).

That is why it makes no sense to "invest" while there is revolving credit or overdraft. Keep only a minimum reserve so as not to go back into debt at the first surprise.
