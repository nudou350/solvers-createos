# Rules for revolving card credit and overdraft in Brazil

Financial education, not professional financial advice. The rules below are Brazilian and are valid as of October 2026; National Monetary Council (CMN) norms and laws change, so ask the user to check bcb.gov.br and planalto.gov.br before big decisions. Average market rates are in another file (average credit rates).

## Revolving card credit: 30 days and then an installment plan

Rule from **Resolução CMN 4.549/2017**:

- When the person pays less than the full bill (for example, only the minimum), the balance goes to **revolving credit** (crédito rotativo).
- The balance can stay in revolving credit only **until the due date of the next bill (about 30 days)**.
- After that, the bank must offer an **installment plan** (parcelamento) for the balance on terms more favorable than revolving credit. The person can also pay everything off.
- Before accepting the installment plan, ask for the installment amount, the number of installments and the **CET** (total effective cost).

What to say: "You cannot leave it in revolving credit for a few months. In one month the debt becomes an installment plan. Pay as much as possible now and compare the CET of the bill installment plan with a personal loan or a payroll loan."

## Card interest cap: 100% of the original amount

**Lei 14.690/2023**, regulated by **Resolução CMN 5.112/2023**, in force since **January 3, 2024**:

- On **revolving credit** and on the **card bill installment plan**, the sum of interest and charges cannot exceed **100% of the original amount of the debt**.
- Example: an original debt of R$ 1,000 can reach, at most, R$ 2,000 in total.
- The cap **does not make the debt cheap**: at 15% a month it doubles in about 5 months. It limits the damage, it does not prevent it.
- It does not apply to personal loans or overdraft, which have their own rules.

Charges above the cap: complain first to the bank's customer service (SAC) and ombudsman, then to consumidor.gov.br and the Central Bank.

## Overdraft: cap of 8% a month

- **Resolução CMN 4.765/2019**, in force since January 6, 2020: overdraft (cheque especial) interest is limited to **8% a month** for individuals and MEIs (individual microentrepreneurs).
- Example: R$ 1,500 used all month costs up to **R$ 120 in interest per month**. In a year of constant use, that is over R$ 1,400 in interest alone.
- Anyone who lives in overdraft should swap it for cheaper installment debt and **lower the limit** so as not to go back to using it.
- It is for emergencies lasting a few days, not for topping up the whole salary every month.

## Swapping expensive debt for cheap debt

- **Credit portability** (portabilidade de crédito): moving a loan or financing to another institution at a lower rate, with no transfer cost. The current bank may make a counteroffer. Ask for the CET of both offers.
- **Payroll-deducted loan** (consignado; deducted from salary or benefit): usually has the lowest rates among personal credit. It commits income for years and you cannot stop paying: use it to swap expensive debt, never for new spending.
- **Secured loan** (against a property or vehicle): lower rate, but the asset is at risk. Only for those with a firm payment plan.
- Golden rule: the swap only pays off if the **new installment fits the budget** and the person **stops using** revolving credit and overdraft. Otherwise it becomes two debts.

## How to calculate what a debt costs

- **Interest for the month ≈ balance × monthly rate.** R$ 3,000 at 15% a month: R$ 450 in interest in one month.
- Compare options by the monthly or annual **CET**, which includes fees, insurance and IOF.
- **Paying only the minimum** on the bill is the most expensive path: the rest goes into revolving credit.
- When renegotiating, ask for it in writing: total amount, number of installments, rate, CET and the discount for paying up front.
