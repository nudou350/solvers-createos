## Type scales

A scale is a sequence of sizes generated by a fixed ratio from a base size (usually 16px). The most used ratios:

| Ratio | Name | Base 16: following sizes | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.125 | major second | 18, 20, 23, 26 | dense interfaces, panels |
| 1.2 | minor third | 19.2, 23, 27.6, 33.2 | products in general |
| 1.25 | major third | 20, 25, 31.3, 39 | products with more personality |
| 1.333 | perfect fourth | 21.3, 28.4, 37.9, 50.5 | content sites, marketing |
| 1.5 | perfect fifth | 24, 36, 54 | landing pages, editorial |

In practice, round to the 4px or 2px grid and fix a short list. A tested, comfortable product scale: **12, 14, 16, 18, 20, 24, 30, 36, 48**. Most screens need only 4 to 6 of these sizes. If the inventory found 9 or more different sizes in a single screen or flow, there is no scale: each designer chose by eye.

On mobile, reduce the ratio (headings that are too big eat the screen): an H1 of 36 to 48px on desktop usually becomes 28 to 32px on mobile. Use typography tokens with a mode per breakpoint or `clamp()` in code.

## Line height

- **Body text:** 1.4 to 1.6 (16px with a 24px line height = 1.5). Long reading text benefits from 1.6.
- **Headings:** 1.1 to 1.3. Large headings at 1.5 look loose and pull the lines of the same heading apart.
- **Dense interfaces (tables, single-line labels):** 1.25 to 1.4 is acceptable.
- Prefer line heights in multiples of 4px (16/24, 14/20, 12/16, 20/28, 24/32, 32/40) to line up with the spacing grid.
- WCAG 1.4.12 (Text Spacing, AA): the layout must not break if the user raises the line height to 1.5, the paragraph spacing to 2x the font size, the letter spacing to 0.12em and the word spacing to 0.16em. In practice: no fixed heights on text containers.

## Measure (line length)

- **45 to 75 characters per line** for running text; 66 is the classic ideal. In CSS, `max-width: 65ch` solves it.
- Below 40 characters, reading becomes choppy (common in narrow mobile columns with a font that is too large). Above 90, the eye gets lost returning to the next line.
- Help messages and descriptions in forms: keep them under 80 characters per line even on desktop.

## Minimum sizes

- **Body on mobile web:** 16px. Below that, iOS Safari applies automatic zoom when focusing form fields with a font smaller than 16px, which breaks the layout.
- **Body on dense desktop:** 14px is acceptable (panels, tables); 16px is still better for long reading.
- **Captions and metadata:** 12px at least, and only for secondary information. Never put essential information (price, deadline, a relevant legal notice) below 14px.
- **All-caps text** (overlines, badges): increase letter spacing (0.04 to 0.08em) and use sparingly; blocks in all caps are read more slowly.

## Weights and families

- 2 to 3 weights are enough: regular (400) for body, medium or semibold (500/600) for labels and emphasis, bold (700) for headings. More weights create noise and increase font loading.
- One family for the interface is usually enough; two (headings and body) at most. System or variable fonts (Inter, Roboto Flex, IBM Plex Sans) reduce loading weight.
- **Avoid thin weights** (100 to 300) in text smaller than 24px: perceived contrast drops a lot, even when the WCAG ratio passes.
- **Tabular numbers** (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) in tables, prices and counters, so digits line up in a column.

## Typographic hierarchy

Hierarchy comes from the combination of size, weight, color and space, not from size alone:

- Neighboring levels need a perceptible difference: at least 20% to 25% size difference, or the same size with a clearly different weight (400 against 600 or more).
- Reducing color contrast (secondary text in gray) creates hierarchy without increasing sizes; but the gray still needs 4.5:1.
- A lean pattern that solves most product screens:
  - **Page title:** 30/36, weight 700.
  - **Section title:** 20/28, weight 600.
  - **Card or subsection title:** 16/24, weight 600.
  - **Body:** 16/24, weight 400.
  - **Secondary/help:** 14/20, weight 400, subtle color.
  - **Caption:** 12/16, weight 400 or 500.
- Avoid two elements at the highest hierarchical level on the same screen. If two texts compete for the top spot, one of them should come down.

## Audit traps

1. **Almost-equal sizes.** 15px and 16px, or 22px and 24px, living on the same screen. That is noise, not hierarchy. Consolidate.
2. **Bold everywhere.** When labels, values and headings are all bold, nothing stands out.
3. **A fixed px line height inherited** from one component to another with a different font (e.g. a 24px line height on 12px text = 2.0, looks too loose).
4. **Aggressive truncation.** Card titles cut to one line hide information; prefer 2 lines with `line-clamp` and the full text accessible.
5. **Sizes in px in code.** Recommend rem (16px = 1rem) to respect the font size the user chose in the browser; WCAG 1.4.4 requires that text can be enlarged up to 200% without loss of content.
6. **Languages other than English.** Words in many languages are on average longer than in English (Portuguese, for example); buttons and tabs designed with English text or lorem ipsum often overflow. Test with the real text in each target language (e.g. Portuguese "Finalizar compra", "Configurações de notificação").

## Quick checklist

1. How many unique sizes? Goal: 4 to 6 per flow, at most 9 in the system.
2. Is there a coherent ratio between them?
3. Body with a line height between 1.4 and 1.6? Headings between 1.1 and 1.3?
4. Running-text measure between 45 and 75 characters?
5. 16px body on mobile and fields at 16px or more?
6. At most 3 weights and no thin weight in small text?
7. Tabular numbers wherever there are numeric columns?
